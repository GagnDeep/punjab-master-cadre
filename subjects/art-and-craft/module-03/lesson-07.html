Rajasthani Miniature Painting

ਪਾਠ 7: ਰਾਜਸਥਾਨੀ ਮਿਨੀਏਚਰ ਚਿੱਤਰਕਾਰੀ

Why this matters (ERB 2022)

You must be able to differentiate the major sub-schools of Rajasthan. For example, ERB consistently tests the elongated figures and 'Bani Thani' of Kishangarh vs. the hunting scenes of Kota.

ਰਾਜਸਥਾਨੀ ਕਲਾ ਵਿੱਚ ਅਲੱਗ-ਅਲੱਗ ਰਿਆਸਤਾਂ ਦੇ ਆਪਣੇ ਸਟਾਈਲ ਸਨ। ਕਿਸ਼ਨਗੜ੍ਹ ਦੀ 'ਬਣੀ ਠਣੀ' (ਜਿਸਨੂੰ ਭਾਰਤ ਦੀ ਮੋਨਾਲੀਸਾ ਕਿਹਾ ਜਾਂਦਾ ਹੈ) ਅਤੇ ਕੋਟਾ ਦੇ ਸ਼ਿਕਾਰ ਦੇ ਦ੍ਰਿਸ਼ਾਂ (Hunting scenes) ਬਾਰੇ ਅਕਸਰ ਪੁੱਛਿਆ ਜਾਂਦਾ ਹੈ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Understand the thematic differences between Mughal (secular/courtly) and Rajasthani (religious/romantic) miniature painting.
  • Identify the characteristics of the Mewar School (bold colors, Sahibdin).
  • Analyze the unique features of the Kishangarh School, specifically the 'Bani Thani' masterpiece.
  • Recognize the prominent themes of the Bundi and Kota schools (nature, hunting).

1. Themes and the Mewar School

While Mughal miniature painting was primarily secular, aristocratic, and focused on chronicling the lives of emperors, Rajasthani painting was deeply rooted in religion, romance, and folk traditions. Heavily influenced by the Bhakti movement, the primary subjects of Rajasthani miniatures were the legends of Radha and Krishna (from the Gita Govinda and Bhagavata Purana), the Ragamala (visual depictions of musical modes), and Nayaka-Nayika Bheda (classifications of romantic heroes and heroines).

The Mewar School (Udaipur) is considered the oldest and most foundational of the Rajasthani styles. It remained relatively free from Mughal influence for a long time. Mewar paintings are characterized by their bold, contrasting primary colors (especially vibrant reds and yellows), flat backgrounds, and prominent, stylized eyes. The figures are often stout and emotionally expressive. The artist Sahibdin (17th century) is the most famous master of the Mewar school, known for his brilliant illustrations of the Rasikapriya and the Ramayana. For the exam, associate 'bold primary colors' and the artist 'Sahibdin' strictly with the Mewar school.

2. The Elegance of Kishangarh: Bani Thani

The Kishangarh School represents the most lyrical, elegant, and romantic phase of Rajasthani painting. Developed in the 18th century under the patronage of King Savant Singh (who wrote poetry under the pen name Nagari Das), the school is entirely defined by its unique facial types and the overarching theme of Radha-Krishna romance, which Savant Singh identified with his own love for his court singer, Bani Thani.

The masterpiece of this school is the portrait of 'Bani Thani', painted by the master artist Nihal Chand. Often referred to as the 'Mona Lisa of India', this painting is a highly frequent topic in ERB exams. The defining characteristics of Kishangarh faces are elongated, slender necks, highly arched eyebrows, sharp, pointed noses and chins, and most notably, the exaggerated, lotus-petal shaped eyes that sweep upwards towards the temple. The backgrounds often feature sweeping panoramic landscapes, lakes with lotus flowers, and gathering storm clouds. Memorizing 'Nihal Chand', 'Bani Thani', and 'elongated facial features' is essential for scoring in this section.

3. Bundi and Kota Schools

The Bundi and Kota schools (often grouped as the Hadoti school) offer a distinct flavor compared to Mewar and Kishangarh. The Bundi school is celebrated for its lush, naturalistic depiction of the environment. Flora, fauna, water bodies, and dramatic night skies are rendered with great affection and detail. The faces in Bundi paintings are typically rounder with prominent shading (an influence of the Mughal style), and there is a frequent use of lush green vegetation and water scenes.

The Kota school, which evolved from Bundi, is famous for one specific theme: the royal hunt (Shikar). While they also painted religious themes, Kota artists excelled in capturing the dynamic action of the hunt in dense, tangled jungles. Tigers, boars, and elephants are depicted with remarkable vitality, often struggling against hunters set amidst thorny thickets and rocky terrain. For examination purposes, the keyword 'Hunting Scenes' (Shikar) should immediately trigger an association with the Kota school.

Worked Example

Q: Which sub-school of Rajasthani Miniature painting is famous for its depiction of elongated faces, lotus-petal shaped eyes, and the masterpiece 'Bani Thani'?

Step 1: Identify the key visual features: 'elongated faces', 'lotus eyes', and the artwork 'Bani Thani'.

Step 2: Match these specific features to the correct Rajasthani sub-school.

Answer: The Kishangarh School.

🧠 How ERB Asks It

ERB frequently uses matching questions for Rajasthani schools. Your mental map should be: Mewar -> Bold Colors/Sahibdin. Kishangarh -> Bani Thani/Nihal Chand/Elongated Eyes. Kota -> Hunting Scenes (Shikar).

Quick Recap

  • Rajasthani painting themes are primarily religious (Radha-Krishna) and romantic (Ragamala), unlike the secular Mughal court art.
  • The Mewar school is known for bold, flat primary colors and the artist Sahibdin.
  • The Kishangarh school, led by artist Nihal Chand, is famous for the 'Bani Thani' portrait and distinctively elongated facial features.
  • The Kota school is uniquely celebrated for its dynamic and detailed hunting scenes (Shikar) set in dense jungles.
