Pahari and Mysore Painting Schools

Master Cadre Art & Craft

Key Point 1

Pahari School ('From the hills'): 17th-19th C. in lower Himalayas (HP/J&K).

Key Point 2

Origins: Mughal artists migrated to hill states during Aurangzeb's reign.

Key Point 3

Themes: Deeply rooted in Vaishnavism (Radha-Krishna romance).

Key Point 4

Basohli School: Early phase. Fierce intensity, bold primary colors.

Key Point 5

Basohli Unique Feature: Use of emerald-green beetle wings to simulate jewelry.

Key Point 6

Kangra School: Classical peak under Raja Sansar Chand. Lyrical grace, delicate lines.

Key Point 7

Master Artist Nainsukh: Bridged the gap between Mughal realism and Pahari aesthetics.

Key Point 8

Mysore School (South): Use of delicate Gesso paste covered with gold leaf for deity jewelry.

Extended Analysis

Pahari schools like Kangra and Basohli brought a lyrical, tender quality to miniature painting, while Mysore painting is renowned for its delicate lines and use of gold leaf.

Historical Timeline Focus

It is crucial to map these developments against contemporary movements globally to fully grasp their significance.

Did you know?

Many artifacts from this period were originally painted in vibrant colors, which have faded over time.
