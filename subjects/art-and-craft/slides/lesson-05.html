Renaissance to Modern Western Art

Master Cadre Art & Craft

Key Point 1

Renaissance ('Rebirth'): Revival of Classical Antiquity (Greece/Rome) in 15th/16th C.

Key Point 2

Humanism: Shift in focus from the purely divine to human potential and anatomy.

Key Point 3

Key Inventions: Linear Perspective (Brunelleschi) and widespread use of Oil Paint.

Key Point 4

Leonardo da Vinci: Mastery of Sfumato (soft, smoky blending) in 'Mona Lisa'.

Key Point 5

Baroque Period (17th C.): Intense emotion, dynamic movement, theatricality.

Key Point 6

Tenebrism: Extreme chiaroscuro (light/dark contrast) pioneered by Caravaggio.

Key Point 7

Impressionism (19th C.): Capturing fleeting light and atmosphere via rapid brushstrokes.

Key Point 8

Plein Air: Impressionists painted outdoors, discarding traditional studio rules.

Quick Recap

  • Review the technical advancements discussed.
  • Ensure memorization of key masterpiece locations.
  • Understand the socio-political context.

Extended Analysis

The Renaissance focus on humanism, perspective, and anatomy revolutionized Western art, paving the way for the dramatic expressions of the Baroque and the innovations of Modern Art.

Historical Timeline Focus

It is crucial to map these developments against contemporary movements globally to fully grasp their significance.

Did you know?

Many artifacts from this period were originally painted in vibrant colors, which have faded over time.
