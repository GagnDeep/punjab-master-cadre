Ajanta and Ellora Cave Paintings

ਪਾਠ 4: ਅਜੰਤਾ ਅਤੇ ਐਲੋਰਾ ਦੀਆਂ ਗੁਫਾਵਾਂ ਦੀ ਕਲਾ

Why this matters (ERB 2022)

Questions often compare the pure Buddhist focus of Ajanta (frescoes, Jataka tales) with the multi-religious nature of Ellora (Hindu, Buddhist, Jain rock-cut architecture like the Kailashnath temple).

ਪੇਪਰਾਂ ਵਿੱਚ ਅਕਸਰ ਅਜੰਤਾ (ਸਿਰਫ ਬੋਧੀ ਧਰਮ, ਚਿੱਤਰਕਾਰੀ) ਅਤੇ ਐਲੋਰਾ (ਹਿੰਦੂ, ਬੋਧੀ, ਜੈਨ ਧਰਮ, ਕੈਲਾਸ਼ਨਾਥ ਮੰਦਰ) ਦੇ ਵਿਚਕਾਰ ਅੰਤਰ ਪੁੱਛੇ ਜਾਂਦੇ ਹਨ। ਇਹਨਾਂ ਦੇ ਵੱਖ-ਵੱਖ ਧਰਮਾਂ ਨਾਲ ਸਬੰਧ ਨੂੰ ਸਮਝਣਾ ਬਹੁਤ ਜ਼ਰੂਰੀ ਹੈ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Differentiate between the thematic focus of Ajanta (Buddhist) and Ellora (Buddhist, Hindu, Jain).
  • Understand the fresco-secco technique used in Ajanta murals.
  • Analyze the significance of Cave 1 at Ajanta (Padmapani and Vajrapani).
  • Appreciate the monolithic rock-cut architecture of the Kailashnath Temple at Ellora (Cave 16).

1. Ajanta Caves: The Zenith of Buddhist Murals

Located in the Sahyadri ranges of Maharashtra, the Ajanta caves comprise 29 rock-cut Buddhist cave monuments. Spanning a period from the 2nd century BCE to about 480 CE, they represent the pinnacle of ancient Indian mural painting. Unlike Ellora, Ajanta is exclusively dedicated to Buddhism. The caves served as Chaityas (prayer halls) and Viharas (monasteries) for Buddhist monks during the monsoons.

The paintings at Ajanta are primarily narrative, depicting scenes from the life of the Buddha and the Jataka tales (stories of his previous incarnations). The technique used is often erroneously called fresco, but it is more accurately described as 'mural' or 'fresco-secco' (painting on dry plaster). The artists first applied a rough layer of clay mixed with cow dung and rice husks, followed by a smooth coat of lime plaster. The paintings were then executed on this dry surface using water-soluble natural pigments. The use of perspective is unique; it employs 'multiple vision', where the viewer's eye is led continuously from one scene to the next without a single focal point.

For the exam, Cave 1 is extremely important. It houses the world-famous murals of the Bodhisattvas Padmapani (holding a lotus) and Vajrapani (holding a thunderbolt). These figures are characterized by their sublime grace, downcast meditative eyes, and exquisite shading that creates a three-dimensional effect (chiaroscuro). The rhythmic, flowing lines and the emotional depth of these paintings are the hallmarks of the Ajanta style.

2. Ellora Caves: A Multi-Religious Tapestry

Situated about 100 kilometers away from Ajanta, the Ellora caves represent a later phase of rock-cut architecture, dating from the 6th to the 11th century CE. While Ajanta is famous for its paintings, Ellora is renowned for its monumental sculpture and architecture. The most crucial distinction for the Master Cadre exam is that Ellora is multi-religious, comprising 34 caves divided into Buddhist (Caves 1-12), Hindu (Caves 13-29), and Jain (Caves 30-34) sections.

The Buddhist caves at Ellora are primarily large, multi-storied Viharas. Cave 10, the Vishvakarma Cave (also known as the Carpenter's Cave), is the only Chaitya hall and is famous for its ribbed ceiling carved in stone to mimic wooden beams, and a large stupa featuring a seated Buddha. The Jain caves, belonging to the Digambara sect, are characterized by detailed, intricate carvings but are smaller in scale compared to the Hindu and Buddhist excavations.

The Hindu caves at Ellora showcase dynamic, powerful sculptures depicting various avatars of Shiva and Vishnu. The imagery here is more dramatic and vigorous than the serene figures of Ajanta. Notable themes include Ravana shaking Mount Kailash and the fierce Dasavatara representations. The proximity of these different religious caves signifies the atmosphere of religious tolerance that prevailed under the Rashtrakuta and Yadava dynasties.

3. The Kailashnath Temple (Ellora Cave 16)

The crown jewel of Ellora is Cave 16, the Kailashnath Temple. It is the largest monolithic structure in the world, meaning it was carved from a single, massive piece of rock. Built by the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I in the 8th century, the temple was excavated top-down; the architects started at the top of the cliff face and excavated downward, leaving the temple structure standing in the center of a massive courtyard.

This architectural marvel is dedicated to Lord Shiva and is designed to resemble Mount Kailash, his mythical abode. The temple complex includes a gateway, a Nandi mandapa, a main hall (gudhamandapa), and the main shrine (garbhagriha) topped by a Dravidian-style vimana (tower). The entire structure, including the towering monolithic elephants and the victory pillars (dhvajastambhas) in the courtyard, was carved from the same bedrock.

From an examination perspective, the Kailashnath temple is highly testable. You must know its location (Ellora Cave 16), its builder (Rashtrakuta king Krishna I), its architectural style (Dravidian monolithic), and its profound sculptural panels. The most famous sculptural relief here is 'Ravana Anugraha Murti' (Ravana shaking Mount Kailash), which vividly captures the tension, power, and dynamic movement characteristic of Ellora's Hindu sculptures.

Worked Example

Q: Which of the following caves is exclusively dedicated to Buddhism and is famous for the fresco-secco murals of Bodhisattva Padmapani?

Step 1: Identify the key identifiers: 'exclusively dedicated to Buddhism' and 'Bodhisattva Padmapani murals'.

Step 2: Recall that Ellora is multi-religious, while Ajanta is purely Buddhist and famous for its paintings.

Answer: Ajanta Cave 1.

🧠 How ERB Asks It

ERB frequently tests the religious affiliation of these sites. Always remember: Ajanta = Only Buddhist. Ellora = Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain. Also, questions on the Kailashnath Temple (Cave 16, Ellora) regarding its monolithic nature and Rashtrakuta patronage are very common.

Quick Recap

  • Ajanta caves are exclusively Buddhist and are celebrated for their intricate mural paintings (fresco-secco).
  • Cave 1 at Ajanta houses the iconic Bodhisattva Padmapani and Vajrapani murals.
  • Ellora caves are multi-religious, featuring Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain excavations.
  • The Kailashnath Temple at Ellora (Cave 16) is a monolithic marvel carved top-down from a single rock.
