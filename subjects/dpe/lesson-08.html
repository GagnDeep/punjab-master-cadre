Lesson 8: Foundations of Psychology

ਪਾਠ 8: ਮਨੋਵਿਗਿਆਨ ਦੀਆਂ ਬੁਨਿਆਦਾਂ

Why this matters (ERB 2018, 2020)

Psychology in sports is heavily tested. Thorndike's Laws of Learning (especially the Law of Effect) and the concepts of intrinsic vs. extrinsic motivation appear in almost every exam.

ਥੌਰਨਡਾਈਕ ਦੇ ਸਿੱਖਣ ਦੇ ਨਿਯਮ ਅਤੇ ਪ੍ਰੇਰਣਾ (Motivation) ਦੇ ਸਵਾਲ ਲਾਜ਼ਮੀ ਆਉਂਦੇ ਹਨ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Define psychology in the context of modern sports.
  • Understand Thorndike's 3 Primary Laws of Learning.
  • Differentiate between Intrinsic and Extrinsic Motivation.
  • Recognize basic personality traits and their impact on athletic performance.

1. What is Sports Psychology?

Historically, psychology was the "study of the soul." Today, modern psychology is defined as the scientific study of behavior and mental processes.

Sports psychology applies these principles to enhance athletic performance, manage anxiety, and improve team dynamics.

2. Thorndike's Laws of Learning

E.L. Thorndike proposed three primary laws of learning that are fundamental to coaching:

  • Law of Readiness: A person must be physically and mentally ready to learn. You cannot force an exhausted or disinterested athlete to learn a new skill effectively.
  • Law of Exercise (or Use/Disuse): Practice makes perfect. Behaviors that are repeated are strengthened; those not practiced are weakened.
  • Law of Effect: Learning is strengthened if it is followed by a satisfying result (reward), and weakened if followed by an annoying result (punishment). This is the most frequently tested law.

3. Motivation

Motivation is the drive to act. In sports, it is divided into two categories:

  • Intrinsic Motivation: Comes from within the athlete. They play for the love of the game, personal satisfaction, or mastery of a skill. (Most durable and powerful form of motivation).
  • Extrinsic Motivation: Comes from outside the athlete. They play for medals, money, fame, or to avoid punishment. While effective in the short term, it can fade if the reward is removed.

4. Personality in Sports

Athletes often exhibit certain traits. Carl Jung divided personalities into two broad categories:

  • Introverts: Quiet, shy, focused inward. Often excel in individual, precision sports (e.g., archery, long-distance running).
  • Extroverts: Outgoing, socially active, seek stimulation. Often excel in fast-paced team sports (e.g., football, basketball).

Worked Example

Q: A coach praises an athlete after they successfully execute a new technique, making the athlete more likely to use that technique again. This demonstrates which of Thorndike's laws?

  1. Law of Readiness
  2. Law of Exercise
  3. Law of Effect
  4. Law of Reaction

Step 1: Identify the action: The athlete receives praise (a satisfying result/reward).

Step 2: The result strengthens the behavior.

Step 3: Match this to the Law of Effect.

Answer: C. Law of Effect.

🧠 How ERB Asks It

They often provide a scenario (like playing to win a cash prize) and ask you to identify the type of motivation. Cash prize = Extrinsic. Playing for self-joy = Intrinsic.

Quick Recap

  • Psychology = Study of behavior and mental processes.
  • Law of Readiness = Must be prepared to learn.
  • Law of Exercise = Practice makes perfect.
  • Law of Effect = Rewards strengthen behavior.
  • Intrinsic = Internal joy; Extrinsic = External rewards.
