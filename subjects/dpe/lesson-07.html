Lesson 7: Digestive & Excretory Systems

ਪਾਠ 7: ਪਾਚਨ ਅਤੇ ਨਿਕਾਸ ਪ੍ਰਣਾਲੀਆਂ

Why this matters (ERB 2017, 2021)

Questions frequently target the location where most digestion happens (Small Intestine) and the primary organ of the excretory system (Kidneys). These are quick, point-scoring questions.

ਸਭ ਤੋਂ ਵੱਧ ਪਾਚਨ ਕਿੱਥੇ ਹੁੰਦਾ ਹੈ (ਛੋਟੀ ਆਂਦਰ) ਅਤੇ ਗੁਰਦਿਆਂ (Kidneys) ਦਾ ਕੀ ਕੰਮ ਹੈ, ਇਹ ਸਵਾਲ ਆਮ ਪੁੱਛੇ ਜਾਂਦੇ ਹਨ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Trace the path of food through the digestive tract.
  • Identify the functions of the stomach, small intestine, and large intestine.
  • Understand the role of the liver and bile.
  • Know the function of the kidneys and nephrons in the excretory system.

1. The Digestive System

Digestion is the process of breaking down food into nutrients that the body can use for energy, growth, and repair.

The Path of Digestion:

  1. Mouth: Chewing (mechanical) and saliva (chemical) begin digestion. Saliva contains the enzyme Amylase which breaks down carbohydrates.
  2. Esophagus: The food pipe. Food moves down via muscular contractions called peristalsis.
  3. Stomach: Churns food with gastric juices (Hydrochloric acid) and enzymes (Pepsin) to break down proteins.
  4. Small Intestine: This is where the majority of digestion and nutrient absorption takes place. It is longer than the large intestine (about 20 feet long!).
  5. Large Intestine (Colon): Absorbs water from the remaining indigestible food matter and forms feces.

2. Accessory Organs

These organs help with digestion but food doesn't pass through them.

  • Liver: The largest internal organ. It produces Bile, which helps break down fats. It also stores glycogen (energy).
  • Gallbladder: Stores the bile produced by the liver until it's needed in the small intestine.
  • Pancreas: Produces digestive enzymes and secretes them into the small intestine. (It also produces insulin, linking it to the endocrine system).

3. The Excretory System

This system removes waste products from the blood and maintains the body's water balance.

  • Kidneys: The primary organs of excretion. They filter blood to remove urea, excess water, and salts, forming urine.
  • Nephron: The microscopic structural and functional unit of the kidney. (Just like the neuron is to the brain, the nephron is to the kidney).
  • Ureters: Tubes carrying urine from kidneys to the bladder.
  • Bladder: Stores urine.
  • Skin: Also an excretory organ (sweat removes water, salts, and small amounts of urea).

Worked Example

Q: In which part of the human digestive system does the maximum absorption of nutrients occur?

  1. Stomach
  2. Large Intestine
  3. Small Intestine
  4. Liver

Step 1: The stomach breaks down food, but doesn't absorb much.

Step 2: The large intestine mainly absorbs water.

Step 3: The small intestine is lined with villi specifically designed for nutrient absorption.

Answer: C. Small Intestine.

🧠 How ERB Asks It

A classic trick question is about Bile: "Where is bile produced?" It is produced by the Liver, but stored in the Gallbladder. Read the question carefully!

Quick Recap

  • Small Intestine = Maximum digestion and absorption.
  • Large Intestine = Water absorption.
  • Liver = Produces Bile. Gallbladder = Stores Bile.
  • Kidneys = Filter blood, create urine.
  • Nephron = Basic filtering unit of the kidney.
