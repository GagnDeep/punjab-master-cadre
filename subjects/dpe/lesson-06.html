Lesson 6: Nervous & Endocrine Systems

ਪਾਠ 6: ਨਾੜੀ ਅਤੇ ਐਂਡੋਕ੍ਰਾਈਨ (ਗ੍ਰੰਥੀ) ਪ੍ਰਣਾਲੀਆਂ

Why this matters (ERB 2018, 2022)

You need to know the 'master gland' (Pituitary) and the fight-or-flight hormone (Adrenaline) for almost every Master Cadre paper. Reaction time questions are also tied to the central nervous system.

ਪਿਟਿਊਟਰੀ ਗ੍ਰੰਥੀ ਅਤੇ ਐਡਰੇਨਾਲੀਨ ਹਾਰਮੋਨ ਬਾਰੇ ਸਵਾਲ ਹਮੇਸ਼ਾ ਪ੍ਰੀਖਿਆ ਵਿੱਚ ਆਉਂਦੇ ਹਨ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Understand the difference between the Central Nervous System (CNS) and Peripheral Nervous System (PNS).
  • Identify the parts of the brain (Cerebrum, Cerebellum, Brain Stem) and their functions in sports.
  • Know the major endocrine glands (Pituitary, Thyroid, Adrenal) and their key hormones.

1. The Nervous System

The nervous system is the body's electrical wiring. It's divided into two main parts:

  • Central Nervous System (CNS): The Brain and the Spinal Cord. This is the command center.
  • Peripheral Nervous System (PNS): All the nerves that branch out from the brain and spinal cord to the rest of the body.

The Brain & Sports

  • Cerebrum: The largest part. Handles conscious thought, memory, and voluntary movement initiation.
  • Cerebellum: The "little brain" at the back. It controls balance, coordination, and fine motor skills. (Crucial for gymnastics, shooting, etc.)
  • Brain Stem (Medulla Oblongata): Controls involuntary functions like breathing and heart rate.

The fundamental unit of the nervous system is the Neuron (nerve cell).

2. The Endocrine System (Glands & Hormones)

While the nervous system uses fast electrical signals, the endocrine system uses slower chemical messengers called hormones released directly into the blood.

  • Pituitary Gland: Known as the "Master Gland" because it controls other glands. It produces Growth Hormone (GH). Located in the brain.
  • Thyroid Gland: Located in the neck. Produces Thyroxine, which regulates the body's metabolism (how we use energy).
  • Adrenal Glands: Located on top of the kidneys. Produces Adrenaline (Epinephrine), the "fight or flight" hormone that increases heart rate and prepares the body for sudden action/sports.
  • Pancreas: Produces Insulin, which regulates blood sugar levels.

Worked Example

Q: Which part of the brain is primarily responsible for maintaining balance and coordinating complex movements?

  1. Cerebrum
  2. Medulla Oblongata
  3. Cerebellum
  4. Hypothalamus

Step 1: Cerebrum is for thought.

Step 2: Medulla is for breathing/heartbeat.

Step 3: Cerebellum (the "little brain") handles balance.

Answer: C. Cerebellum.

🧠 How ERB Asks It

Expect a direct 1-to-1 match question: Pituitary = Master Gland. Adrenaline = Emergency/Fight or Flight. Thyroid = Metabolism.

Quick Recap

  • CNS = Brain + Spinal Cord.
  • Cerebellum controls balance and coordination.
  • Neuron is the basic unit of the nervous system.
  • Pituitary = Master Gland (Growth).
  • Adrenal Gland = Adrenaline (Fight or flight).
