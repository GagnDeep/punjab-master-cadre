Lesson 5: Circulatory & Respiratory Systems

ਪਾਠ 5: ਸੰਚਾਰ ਅਤੇ ਸਾਹ ਪ੍ਰਣਾਲੀਆਂ

Why this matters (ERB 2019, 2021)

Questions about the heart's chambers, blood pressure norms (120/80), and terms like "Stroke Volume" or "Vital Capacity" are very frequent. Understanding how oxygen gets to muscles is core to sports science.

ਦਿਲ ਦੇ ਚੈਂਬਰਾਂ, ਬਲੱਡ ਪ੍ਰੈਸ਼ਰ (120/80), ਅਤੇ ਫੇਫੜਿਆਂ ਦੀ ਸਮਰੱਥਾ ਬਾਰੇ ਸਵਾਲ ਅਕਸਰ ਆਉਂਦੇ ਹਨ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Understand the flow of blood through the 4 chambers of the heart.
  • Differentiate between arteries, veins, and capillaries.
  • Define Stroke Volume and Cardiac Output.
  • Understand the mechanics of breathing and define Vital Capacity.

1. The Circulatory System (Heart & Blood)

The human heart has 4 chambers: Right Atrium, Right Ventricle, Left Atrium, Left Ventricle. The primary job is to pump oxygenated blood to the body and deoxygenated blood to the lungs.

Blood Vessels

  • Arteries: Carry oxygenated blood AWAY from the heart to the body (Exception: Pulmonary Artery carries deoxygenated blood to lungs). They have thick, elastic walls.
  • Veins: Carry deoxygenated blood back TO the heart (Exception: Pulmonary Vein). They have valves to prevent backflow.
  • Capillaries: Tiny vessels where gas exchange (oxygen ↔ carbon dioxide) occurs at the muscle level.

Key Cardiovascular Terms

  • Heart Rate (HR): Beats per minute (Average resting is 72 bpm).
  • Stroke Volume (SV): The amount of blood pumped out of the heart (left ventricle) per beat.
  • Cardiac Output (CO): The total amount of blood pumped per minute.
    Formula: CO = HR × SV
  • Blood Pressure: Normal is 120 (Systolic - contracting) / 80 (Diastolic - relaxing) mmHg.

2. The Respiratory System (Lungs)

The primary function is gas exchange: bringing Oxygen (O2) in and expelling Carbon Dioxide (CO2) out.

Path of Air: Nose/Mouth → Trachea (windpipe) → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli (tiny air sacs where gas exchange happens with capillaries).

Key Respiratory Terms

  • Tidal Volume: The normal amount of air breathed in and out during rest.
  • Vital Capacity: The maximum amount of air a person can exhale after a maximum inhalation. It's a great indicator of respiratory fitness.
  • Second Wind: A phenomenon in endurance sports where an athlete feels sudden relief from fatigue and breathing becomes easier after initial distress. It happens when the body's oxygen supply catches up with the demand.

Worked Example

Q: Which blood vessels carry blood away from the heart?

  1. Veins
  2. Capillaries
  3. Arteries
  4. Vena Cava

Step 1: Remember the mnemonic: Arteries = Away.

Step 2: Veins bring blood back.

Answer: C. Arteries.

🧠 How ERB Asks It

Examiners love exceptions. The Pulmonary Artery is the ONLY artery that carries deoxygenated blood. The Pulmonary Vein is the ONLY vein that carries oxygenated blood.

Quick Recap

  • Arteries = Away from heart (usually oxygenated).
  • Veins = Back to heart (usually deoxygenated, have valves).
  • Cardiac Output = Heart Rate × Stroke Volume.
  • Alveoli = Tiny sacs where gas exchange occurs in lungs.
  • Vital Capacity = Max air exhaled after max inhalation.
