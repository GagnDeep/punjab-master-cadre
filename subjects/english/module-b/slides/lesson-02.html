Tagore & Sarojini Naidu

The Golden Voices of Indian Poetry

Rabindranath Tagore (1861–1941)

  • Polymath: Poet, novelist, musician, painter, and philosopher.
  • First non-European to win the Nobel Prize in Literature (1913).
  • Wrote the national anthems for India and Bangladesh.
  • Founded Visva-Bharati University in Santiniketan.

Gitanjali (Song Offerings)

  • Originally published in Bengali in 1910.
  • English translation published in 1912 by the India Society of London.
  • Famous introduction written by the Irish poet W.B. Yeats.
  • Deeply spiritual prose poems expressing devotion to God.

Themes of Gitanjali

Tagore's Other Major Works

  • Poetry: The Crescent Moon, The Gardener
  • Plays: Chitra, The Post Office, Red Oleanders
  • Novels: Gora, The Home and the World (Ghare-Baire)

Sarojini Naidu (1879–1949)

  • Known as the 'Nightingale of India' (Bharat Kokila).
  • Prominent freedom fighter and close associate of Mahatma Gandhi.
  • First Indian woman to become President of the Indian National Congress (1925).

The English Poet with an Indian Soul

The Advice

Early in her career, British poet Sir Edmund Gosse advised her to stop imitating English poets and write about Indian themes, landscapes, and people.

The Result

Her poetry became a vibrant canvas of Indian weavers, bangle sellers, palanquin bearers, and snake charmers.

Naidu's Poetry Collections

  • The Golden Threshold (1905) - Her first collection.
  • The Bird of Time (1912) - Includes "In the Bazaars of Hyderabad".
  • The Broken Wing (1917) - Explores sorrow, death, and Indian nationalism.
  • The Sceptered Flute (1928) - Collected poems.

Famous Poems

  • "The Bangle Sellers" - Describes the stages of an Indian woman's life through the colors of her bangles.
  • "Palanquin Bearers" - Captures the rhythmic movement and song of the bearers carrying a royal bride.
"Lightly, O lightly we bear her along,
She sways like a flower in the wind of our song..."
- Palanquin Bearers
