The Founding Trio

Narayan, Rao, and Anand

Who are the "Founding Trio"?

  • Pioneers of the Indian English Novel in the 1930s.
  • R.K. Narayan, Mulk Raj Anand, Raja Rao.
  • They gave Indian English literature a distinct, indigenous identity.
  • Moved away from purely British imitations to Indian themes and sensibilities.

R.K. Narayan: The Creator of Malgudi

  • Known for his fictional South Indian town: Malgudi.
  • Style: Simple, unpretentious, with gentle humor.
  • Focuses on middle-class life, tradition vs. modernity.

Narayan's Major Works

Early Classics

  • Swami and Friends (1935)
  • The Bachelor of Arts (1937)
  • The English Teacher (1945)

Later Masterpieces

  • The Guide (1958) - Sahitya Akademi Award
  • The Man-Eater of Malgudi (1961)
  • The Vendor of Sweets (1967)

Visualizing Malgudi

Mulk Raj Anand: The Social Realist

  • Heavily influenced by Marxism and the Indian independence movement.
  • Focused on the marginalized, poor, and oppressed classes.
  • Highly critical of the caste system, religious hypocrisy, and British colonialism.

Anand's Key Novels

The Masterpiece

  • Untouchable (1935)
  • Protagonist: Bakha, a young sweeper.
  • Intro by E.M. Forster.

Other Works

  • Coolie (1936) - Munoo's tragic journey.
  • Two Leaves and a Bud (1937) - Exploitation in Assam tea estates.

Raja Rao: The Philosophical Voice

  • Deeply influenced by Advaita Vedanta and Gandhian philosophy.
  • Explored the East-West cultural encounter and Indian identity.
  • Created a unique English prose style capturing the rhythm of Indian speech.

Rao's Key Novels

Kanthapura (1938)

  • Impact of Gandhi's freedom struggle on a South Indian village.
  • Narrated in the style of an Indian 'Sthala-Purana' (local legend).

The Serpent and the Rope (1960)

  • Highly autobiographical and philosophical.
  • Explores the concept of illusion (Serpent) vs. reality (Rope) in an East-West marriage.
