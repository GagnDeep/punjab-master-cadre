Ramanujan, Khushwant Singh & Others

Poetry of Memory & Literature of Partition

A.K. Ramanujan (1929–1993)

  • Poet, translator, folklorist, and philologist.
  • Wrote in English and Kannada.
  • Spent much of his academic life at the University of Chicago, leading to a strong sense of expatriate nostalgia.

Ramanujan's Poetic Themes

Core Motifs

  • Memory & Family: Recalling childhood, relatives, and Hindu traditions from a distance.
  • Irony & Objectivity: A detached, unsentimental look at Indian culture.

Key Poems

  • "A River" - Critiques poets who romanticize floods while ignoring human tragedy.
  • "Obituary" - An ironic reflection on his father's death.

The Dual Worlds of Ramanujan

Khushwant Singh (1915–2014)

  • Lawyer, journalist, politician, and novelist.
  • Known for his trenchant secularism, humor, and profound knowledge of Sikh history.
  • Witnessed the horrors of the 1947 Partition firsthand, which deeply influenced his masterpiece.

Train to Pakistan (1956)

The Setting

  • Mano Majra: A fictional village on the border of India and Pakistan.
  • Historically peaceful, where Sikhs and Muslims lived in harmony.

The Narrative

  • A "ghost train" full of corpses arrives, shattering the peace.
  • Explores the failure of government/police (Iqbal, the magistrate) vs. the sacrifice of an outlaw (Juggut Singh) for love.

Other Masters: Nissim Ezekiel

  • Considered the father of post-independence Indian English poetry.
  • Style: Colloquial, ironic, focused on urban alienation (especially in Bombay).
  • Famous Poem: "Night of the Scorpion" - contrasts the mother's selfless love with the villagers' superstitions and the father's rationalism.

Other Masters: Kamala Das

  • Pioneered confessional poetry in India.
  • Wrote openly about female sexuality, domestic oppression, and the quest for identity.
  • Famous Poem: "An Introduction" ("I am Indian, very brown, born in Malabar...").
  • Autobiography: My Story (caused a sensation upon release).

Other Masters: Shashi Deshpande

Themes

Focuses on the suffocating nature of middle-class Indian marriages and the silencing of women's voices.

Major Work

That Long Silence (1988)
Won the Sahitya Akademi Award. The story of Jaya, a writer who suppresses her true self to maintain a "happy" marriage.
