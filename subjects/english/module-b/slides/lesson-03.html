Modern Novelists

Desai, Rushdie, Roy, and Ghosh

Anita Desai

  • Pioneer of the psychological novel in India.
  • Focuses on the inner lives, emotional trauma, and alienation of her characters.
  • Moves away from broad social commentary to intense personal struggles.

Desai's Major Works

Key Novels

  • Cry, the Peacock (1963) - Story of Maya's obsessive fear and psychological breakdown.
  • Clear Light of Day (1980) - Tensions between siblings in Old Delhi.
  • In Custody (1984) - A Hindi teacher's disastrous attempt to interview a famous Urdu poet.

Note for Exam: Anita Desai's novels frequently deal with marital incompatibility and the existential angst of middle-class women.

Salman Rushdie

  • Redefined the Indian English novel in 1981.
  • Master of Magic Realism (blending magical elements into a realistic atmosphere).
  • Known for exuberant wordplay, historical allegories, and post-colonial themes.

Midnight's Children (1981)

Arundhati Roy

  • Architect turned author and social activist.
  • Known for highly lyrical, unconventional prose and non-linear narrative structures.
  • Fierce critic of the caste system, globalization, and state violence.

The God of Small Things (1997)

  • Award: Won the Booker Prize (1997).
  • Setting: Ayemenem, Kerala.
  • Plot: The tragic story of twins Estha and Rahel, and their mother Ammu's forbidden affair with Velutha (an untouchable).
Key Motif

The "Love Laws" – unwritten social rules that dictate "who should be loved, and how. And how much."

Amitav Ghosh

  • Novelist with an anthropologist's eye.
  • Known for deep historical research and sweeping narratives.
  • Explores themes of colonialism, migration, artificial borders, and recently, climate change.
  • First English writer to win the Jnanpith Award (2018).

Ghosh's Major Works

The Shadow Lines (1988)

  • Examines the absurdity of national borders (specifically the partition of Bengal).
  • Sahitya Akademi Award winner.

The Ibis Trilogy

  • Sea of Poppies, River of Smoke, Flood of Fire.
  • Epic historical fiction set during the build-up to the Opium Wars.

The Four Pillars of Modern Fiction

Desai: The Inner Mind
Rushdie: Magic & Myth
Roy: Caste & Society
Ghosh: History & Borders
