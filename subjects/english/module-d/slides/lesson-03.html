Morphology & Word Formation

What is Morphology?

  • The study of the internal structure of words.
  • Explains how words are formed from smaller units.
  • Closely related to syntax and phonology.

Morphemes

  • The smallest meaningful linguistic unit.
  • Unlike a phoneme, a morpheme has semantic meaning.
  • Words can consist of one morpheme (cat) or many (un-friend-li-ness).

Free vs. Bound Morphemes

  • Free Morphemes: Can stand alone as independent words (e.g., dog, run, happy).
  • Bound Morphemes: Cannot stand alone; must be attached to another morpheme (e.g., -s, un-, -ed).

Lexical vs. Functional

  • Lexical Morphemes: Carry the main content/meaning (nouns, verbs, adjectives). 'Open class'.
  • Functional Morphemes: Serve grammatical purposes (conjunctions, prepositions, articles). 'Closed class'.

Inflectional Morphemes

  • Bound morphemes that indicate grammatical function.
  • Never change the grammatical category (word class).
  • English has 8: -s (pl), -'s (poss), -s (3rd per sing), -ing, -ed, -en, -er (comp), -est (super).

Derivational Morphemes

  • Bound morphemes used to create new words.
  • Often change the grammatical category of the base word.
  • Example: 'teach' (verb) + '-er' = 'teacher' (noun).

Compounding & Blending

  • Compounding: Joining two free morphemes (blackboard, sunflower).
  • Blending: Combining parts of two words (smog = smoke + fog; brunch = breakfast + lunch).

Clipping & Acronyms

  • Clipping: Shortening a longer word (ad, math, flu).
  • Acronyms: Words formed from initial letters, pronounced as a word (NASA, laser).
  • Initialisms: Initial letters pronounced separately (FBI, VIP).

Backformation & Conversion

  • Backformation: A word is formed by removing a supposed affix (editor -> edit).
  • Conversion: Changing word class without adding affixes (noun 'google' -> verb 'to google').

Exam Tips

  • Master the 8 inflectional morphemes.
  • Be able to dissect words into their constituent morphemes.
  • Identify the word formation process for given examples.
