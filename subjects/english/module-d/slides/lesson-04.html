Syntax & Phrase Structure Rules

Syntax Basics

  • The study of the rules governing sentence structure.
  • How words combine to form phrases and sentences.
  • Ensures grammatical correctness independent of meaning.

Phrase Structure Rules

  • Rules that describe the internal structure of sentences.
  • S -> NP VP (A sentence consists of a Noun Phrase and a Verb Phrase).
  • Generates the hierarchical structure of language.

Syntax Tree Diagram

Noun Phrase (NP)

  • A phrase built around a Noun head.
  • Rule: NP -> (Det) (Adj) N (PP).
  • Example: 'The quick brown fox'.

Verb Phrase (VP)

  • A phrase built around a Verb head.
  • Rule: VP -> V (NP) (PP).
  • Example: 'slept under the tree'.

Prepositional Phrase (PP)

  • A phrase consisting of a Preposition and a Noun Phrase.
  • Rule: PP -> P NP.
  • Example: 'in the house', 'on the table'.

Structural Ambiguity

  • When a single sentence has multiple valid underlying structures.
  • Results in two or more distinct meanings.
  • Example: 'I saw the man with the telescope.'

Recursion

  • The ability to embed phrases within phrases indefinitely.
  • Allows human language to express infinite ideas.
  • Example: 'The cat that chased the mouse that ate the cheese...'

Deep vs. Surface Structure

  • Deep Structure: The abstract, underlying semantic meaning.
  • Surface Structure: The actual syntactic form that is spoken.
  • Transformations map deep to surface.

Exam Tips

  • Know S -> NP VP by heart.
  • Be able to identify phrase types within a sentence.
  • Understand how ambiguity relates to tree structures.
