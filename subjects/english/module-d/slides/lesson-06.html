Socio- & Psycholinguistics

Sociolinguistics Overview

  • The study of how language interacts with society.
  • Examines variables like class, gender, geography, and ethnicity.
  • Focuses on language variation and change.

Dialect vs. Accent

  • Dialect: Variation in vocabulary, grammar, AND pronunciation based on region or social group.
  • Accent: Variation in pronunciation ONLY.
  • 'A language is a dialect with an army and a navy.'

Register & Jargon

  • Register: Language variety determined by context/formality (e.g., formal vs. casual).
  • Jargon: Specialized vocabulary used by specific professions (e.g., legal, medical).
  • Slang: Informal, ephemeral vocabulary used by social groups.

Pidgin

  • A simplified contact language used by groups who share no common language.
  • Has NO native speakers.
  • Simplified grammar, limited vocabulary.

Creole

  • A pidgin that has become the first language (native tongue) of a new generation.
  • Develops a complex grammar and expanded vocabulary.
  • Process is called 'Creolization'.

Psycholinguistics Overview

  • The study of the psychological and neurobiological factors of language.
  • Focuses on language comprehension, production, and acquisition.

Language Acquisition Device (LAD)

  • Proposed by Chomsky.
  • A hypothetical module in the human mind pre-programmed to process language.
  • Allows children to extract grammatical rules from speech.

Critical Period Hypothesis

  • Proposed by Eric Lenneberg.
  • There is a biologically determined window (early childhood to puberty) for native-like language acquisition.
  • After this period, language learning becomes difficult and rarely reaches native fluency.

Broca's and Wernicke's Areas

  • Broca's Area: Located in the frontal lobe; responsible for language production. (Broca's aphasia = halting speech).
  • Wernicke's Area: Located in the temporal lobe; responsible for language comprehension. (Wernicke's aphasia = fluent but nonsensical speech).

Exam Tips

  • Know the progression: Pidgin (no native speakers) -> Creole (native speakers).
  • Register depends on context/setting.
  • LAD is innate; Critical Period restricts perfect learning by age.
