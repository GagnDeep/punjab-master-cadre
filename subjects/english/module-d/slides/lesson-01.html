Phonetics: Organs of Speech & Sounds

Phonetics Definition

  • The scientific study of speech sounds.
  • Articulatory Phonetics: How sounds are produced.
  • Acoustic Phonetics: Physical properties of sound waves.
  • Auditory Phonetics: How sounds are perceived.

The Vocal Tract

  • Includes all air passages above the larynx.
  • Oral cavity (mouth) and Nasal cavity (nose).
  • Speech is primarily produced on egressive pulmonic airflow (breathing out).

The Vocal Tract

Active Articulators

  • Organs that move to produce sound.
  • Tongue: The most active articulator (tip, blade, front, back).
  • Lower Lip: Moves to meet the upper lip or upper teeth.
  • Velum: Moves to open/close the nasal passage.

Passive Articulators

  • Stationary organs that active articulators touch.
  • Upper Teeth: Used for dental sounds like /θ/, /ð/.
  • Alveolar Ridge: The hard ridge behind upper teeth.
  • Hard Palate: The bony roof of the mouth.

The Glottis

  • The space between the vocal cords in the larynx.
  • Open Glottis: Produces voiceless sounds (e.g., /p/, /t/, /s/).
  • Narrow Glottis: Produces voiced sounds (e.g., /b/, /d/, /z/).
  • Closed Glottis: Produces a glottal stop.

Consonant Classification

  • Place of Articulation: Where the sound is made (e.g., Bilabial, Alveolar).
  • Manner of Articulation: How the airflow is restricted (e.g., Stops, Fricatives).
  • Voicing: Vibration of vocal cords (Voiced vs. Voiceless).

Vowel Classification

  • Produced with relatively open vocal tract.
  • Classified by Tongue Height (High, Mid, Low).
  • Classified by Tongue Advancement (Front, Central, Back).
  • Classified by Lip Rounding (Rounded vs. Unrounded).

Monophthongs vs Diphthongs

  • Monophthongs: Pure vowels with a single target articulation (12 in English).
  • Diphthongs: Gliding vowels that move from one position to another (8 in English).
  • Example: /aɪ/ in 'buy'.

Summary for Exams

  • Always remember: 44 phonemes = 24 consonants + 20 vowels.
  • Velum controls nasal vs. oral sounds.
  • Identify active vs. passive articulators for direct questions.
