Chomsky's Transformational Generative Grammar

Noam Chomsky

  • The 'Father of Modern Linguistics'.
  • Published 'Syntactic Structures' in 1957.
  • Shifted focus from descriptive to generative grammar.

Transformational Generative Grammar

  • TGG: A system of rules that can generate all and only the grammatical sentences of a language.
  • Proposes that language is governed by innate rules.

Competence vs. Performance

  • Competence: The idealized, unconscious knowledge of grammar rules held by a native speaker.
  • Performance: The actual use of language in concrete situations, susceptible to errors and memory limits.

Deep Structure

  • The underlying, abstract level of structural organization.
  • Determines the semantic interpretation (meaning) of a sentence.
  • Generated by phrase structure rules.

Surface Structure

  • The phonetic form of the sentence as it is actually spoken or written.
  • Derived from deep structure via transformations.

Transformational Rules

  • Operations that change deep structures into surface structures.
  • Can involve movement, insertion, or deletion of elements.
  • Explains active-passive relationships and question formation.

Universal Grammar (UG)

  • The hypothesis that all human languages share a common underlying structure.
  • Innate biological endowment specific to humans.
  • Explains how children learn language so quickly despite limited input.

Poverty of the Stimulus

  • Chomsky's argument for innate grammar.
  • The language children hear is fragmented and incomplete, yet they develop perfect grammar.
  • Conclusion: Language ability must be innate.

Chomsky vs. Behaviorism

  • Chomsky famously critiqued B.F. Skinner's 'Verbal Behavior'.
  • Argued language is not merely learned through imitation and reinforcement.
  • Language involves creativity (generating novel sentences).

Exam Tips

  • Distinguish Competence (knowledge) from Performance (use).
  • Deep structure = Meaning; Surface structure = Form.
  • UG is hardwired into the human brain.
