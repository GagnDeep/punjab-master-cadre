15. Modern Age II: Yeats, Shaw, Beckett

ਆਧੁਨਿਕ ਯੁੱਗ 2

Why this matters (ERB Pattern)

Match the playwright to the movement! Bernard Shaw = Drama of Ideas (Problem Plays). Samuel Beckett = Theatre of the Absurd. Yeats = Irish Literary Revival.

ਪ੍ਰੀਖਿਆ ਵਿੱਚ 'Drama of Ideas' ਅਤੇ 'Theatre of the Absurd' ਦੇ ਲੇਖਕਾਂ ਬਾਰੇ ਲਾਜ਼ਮੀ ਸਵਾਲ ਆਉਂਦਾ ਹੈ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Analyze W.B. Yeats's use of symbolism and his prophetic vision in 'The Second Coming'.
  • Define G.B. Shaw's 'Drama of Ideas' and the plot of Pygmalion.
  • Understand the philosophy behind the 'Theatre of the Absurd' and Beckett's 'Waiting for Godot'.

1. W.B. Yeats (1865–1939)

William Butler Yeats was an Irish poet and one of the foremost figures of 20th-century literature. He was a driving force behind the Irish Literary Revival and a founder of the Abbey Theatre. Unlike Eliot, whose modernism was urban and fragmented, Yeats's early work was deeply rooted in Irish mythology and Symbolism. Later in life, his poetry became starker, more political, and deeply mystical, developing a complex personal system of astrology and history (published as A Vision).

  • The Second Coming (1920): A terrifying, prophetic poem written in the aftermath of WWI and the Russian Revolution. It envisions the end of the Christian era and the arrival of a brutal, monstrous new historical cycle. (Famous lines: "Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold," and "What rough beast... Slouches towards Bethlehem to be born?")
  • Sailing to Byzantium (1928): A profound poem about aging, the decay of the physical body, and the desire to escape into the eternal, unchanging world of art (symbolized by the ancient city of Byzantium).
  • Easter, 1916: A poem commemorating the Irish nationalist uprising against the British, famous for the refrain: "A terrible beauty is born."

2. George Bernard Shaw (1856–1950)

G.B. Shaw was a brilliant Irish playwright, critic, and staunch socialist (a member of the Fabian Society). He revolutionized the English stage by creating the Drama of Ideas (or the Problem Play). He despised the artificial, melodramatic plays of the Victorian era. Instead, he used the stage as a platform for debate, social critique, and intellectual discussion, heavily influenced by the Norwegian playwright Henrik Ibsen.

  • Pygmalion (1913): His most famous play. A phonetician, Henry Higgins, makes a bet that he can pass off a cockney flower girl, Eliza Doolittle, as a duchess simply by teaching her upper-class English. It is a biting satire on the British class system and superficial social judgments (later adapted into the musical My Fair Lady).
  • Arms and the Man (1894): A brilliant anti-romantic comedy that satirizes the glorification of war and the romanticized view of soldiers.
  • Man and Superman (1903): A massive, philosophical play exploring Friedrich Nietzsche's concept of the "Übermensch" and Shaw's own theory of the "Life Force."

3. Samuel Beckett (1906–1989)

Samuel Beckett was an avant-garde Irish novelist and playwright. He is the central figure of the Theatre of the Absurd. This post-WWII movement stripped theatre down to its bare minimum. The plays feature meaningless dialogue, repetitive actions, and a lack of traditional plot, reflecting the existential belief that human existence has no inherent purpose or meaning.

  • Waiting for Godot (1953): The defining masterpiece of Absurdist theatre. Two tramps, Vladimir and Estragon, wait endlessly on a barren road by a single tree for a man named Godot, who never arrives. It is famously described as "a play in which nothing happens, twice."

Worked Example

Q: Which play by Samuel Beckett is famously described as a masterpiece of the 'Theatre of the Absurd' where two characters wait endlessly for someone who never arrives?

Step 1: Recall the definition of the Theatre of the Absurd (lack of plot, existential meaninglessness).

Step 2: Identify Samuel Beckett as its primary figure.

Step 3: Match him to his masterpiece about endless waiting.

Step 4: Select 'Waiting for Godot'.

🧠 How ERB Asks It

Quotes from Yeats's 'The Second Coming' ('Things fall apart...') are very common. Also, remember that Pygmalion is a satire on the *class system* and language, not a romance.

Quick Recap

  • W.B. Yeats: Irish poet. Used Symbolism. Key works: The Second Coming (prophetic, 'Things fall apart'), Sailing to Byzantium.
  • G.B. Shaw: Created the 'Drama of Ideas' (focus on intellectual debate, social critique).
  • Pygmalion: Shaw's masterpiece. Higgins teaches Eliza Doolittle to speak upper-class English. Satirizes the class system.
  • Samuel Beckett: 'Theatre of the Absurd' (existential meaninglessness).
  • Waiting for Godot: Beckett's masterpiece. Two tramps wait for a man who never comes.
