13. Later Victorians: Dickens, Hardy, Eliot, Arnold

ਬਾਅਦ ਦੇ ਵਿਕਟੋਰੀਅਨ

Why this matters (ERB Pattern)

Hardy's fictional setting ('Wessex') and George Eliot's real name ('Mary Ann Evans') are very common trivia questions on the ERB exam.

ਪ੍ਰੀਖਿਆ ਵਿੱਚ ਥਾਮਸ ਹਾਰਡੀ ਦਾ ਇਲਾਕਾ 'Wessex' ਅਤੇ ਜੌਰਜ ਇਲੀਅਟ ਦਾ ਅਸਲੀ ਨਾਮ ਪੁੱਛਿਆ ਜਾਂਦਾ ਹੈ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Analyze Charles Dickens's use of social realism and serialized publication.
  • Understand Thomas Hardy's philosophy of Determinism/Fatalism and his fictional setting of Wessex.
  • Identify George Eliot's real name and her masterpiece 'Middlemarch'.
  • Recall Matthew Arnold's 'Dover Beach' as the ultimate expression of Victorian religious doubt.

1. Later Victorians: The Zenith of the Novel

As the Victorian era progressed, the novel became the dominant literary form. While early Victorian literature often wrestled with abstract ideas like faith vs. science, later Victorian literature became highly focused on Social Realism—documenting the gritty realities of industrialization, poverty, class struggle, and the hypocrisy of social institutions.

2. Charles Dickens (1812–1870)

Charles Dickens was the most popular novelist of the Victorian era. His novels were often published in monthly serial installments, creating a massive, eager reading public across all classes. He used his fiction to campaign fiercely against social injustices, particularly child labor, terrible schooling, and the brutal legal system.

Key Characteristics of Dickens's Writing:

  • Social Critique: Attacks on institutions like the workhouse (Oliver Twist), the Chancery Court (Bleak House), and utilitarian education (Hard Times).
  • Caricatures: Creating memorable, often grotesque characters defined by a single physical or verbal quirk (e.g., Mr. Micawber, Uriah Heep, Miss Havisham).
  • Sentimentality: Scenes of extreme, tear-jerking emotion, especially involving the deaths of innocent children (e.g., Little Nell).

Major Works:

  • David Copperfield (1850): His most autobiographical novel.
  • Great Expectations (1861): A masterpiece tracing the moral education of the orphan Pip, who receives money from a mysterious benefactor (the convict Magwitch).
  • A Tale of Two Cities (1859): A historical novel set during the French Revolution ("It was the best of times, it was the worst of times...").

3. Thomas Hardy (1840–1928)

Thomas Hardy represents the darker, pessimistic end of the Victorian era. Influenced by Darwinism and the decline of rural life, Hardy's novels are set in the fictional, semi-mythical region of Wessex (based on southwestern England).

His philosophy was essentially Fatalism or Determinism: the belief that humans are victims of an indifferent, uncaring universe, blind chance, and their own uncontrollable passions. There is rarely a happy ending in Hardy's major works.

  • Tess of the d'Urbervilles (1891): The tragic story of a pure, innocent peasant girl destroyed by society's double standards and blind fate.
  • Jude the Obscure (1895): A devastating critique of marriage and the university system. The public outcry against its bleakness and attacks on religion was so fierce that Hardy stopped writing novels entirely and spent the rest of his life writing poetry.
  • The Mayor of Casterbridge (1886): A man sells his wife and daughter in a drunken fit, leading to his eventual downfall.

4. George Eliot (1819–1880)

George Eliot was the pen name of Mary Ann Evans. She used a male pseudonym to ensure her works were taken seriously. She pioneered psychological realism in the English novel, focusing intensely on the internal motivations and moral choices of her characters in provincial settings.

  • Middlemarch (1871-72): Subtitled "A Study of Provincial Life," it is widely considered one of the greatest novels in the English language. It features a vast web of interconnected characters, focusing on the idealistic but naive Dorothea Brooke and the ambitious doctor Tertius Lydgate.

5. Matthew Arnold (1822–1888)

Matthew Arnold was a poet and a highly influential cultural critic. As a poet, his most famous work is Dover Beach, a profoundly melancholic poem lamenting the "melancholy, long, withdrawing roar" of the Sea of Faith, capturing the era's loss of religious certainty. As a critic, he wrote Culture and Anarchy, arguing that high culture ("the best which has been thought and said") is the only defense against the growing materialism and anarchy of the industrial age.

Worked Example

Q: The fictional, semi-mythical region of 'Wessex' serves as the primary setting for the novels of which Victorian author?

Step 1: Identify the term 'Wessex'.

Step 2: Recall that it is a rural, agricultural setting.

Step 3: Associate it with the pessimistic novels of the late Victorian era.

Step 4: Select Thomas Hardy.

🧠 How ERB Asks It

Pay attention to opening lines! Dickens's 'It was the best of times...' is from 'A Tale of Two Cities', NOT 'Oliver Twist' or 'Great Expectations'.

Quick Recap

  • Charles Dickens: Social Realism. Campaigned against poverty/injustice. Works published in serials. Key novels: Great Expectations, David Copperfield, A Tale of Two Cities.
  • Thomas Hardy: Fatalism/Determinism (pessimistic worldview). Setting: Wessex. Key novels: Tess of the d'Urbervilles, Jude the Obscure.
  • George Eliot: Real name: Mary Ann Evans. Masterpiece: Middlemarch (Psychological realism).
  • Matthew Arnold: Wrote 'Dover Beach', mourning the loss of religious faith.

ਡਿਕਨਜ਼ ਦੀਆਂ ਕਹਾਣੀਆਂ ਅਖਬਾਰਾਂ ਵਿੱਚ ਕਿਸ਼ਤਾਂ (serial installments) ਵਿੱਚ ਛਪਦੀਆਂ ਸਨ। ਉਸਦੇ ਪਾਤਰ ਬਹੁਤ ਹੀ ਅਜੀਬ ਪਰ ਯਾਦ ਰਹਿਣ ਵਾਲੇ ਹੁੰਦੇ ਸਨ (ਜਿਵੇਂ Great Expectations ਵਿੱਚ ਮਿਸ ਹੈਵਿਸ਼ਮ - Miss Havisham)। ਉਸਦਾ ਨਾਵਲ A Tale of Two Cities ਫਰਾਂਸ ਦੀ ਕ੍ਰਾਂਤੀ 'ਤੇ ਆਧਾਰਿਤ ਹੈ, ਜਿਸਦੀ ਪਹਿਲੀ ਲਾਈਨ ਬਹੁਤ ਮਸ਼ਹੂਰ ਹੈ: "It was the best of times, it was the worst of times..." ਇਸ ਤੋਂ ਬਾਅਦ ਆਉਂਦਾ ਹੈ ਥਾਮਸ ਹਾਰਡੀ (Thomas Hardy)। ਹਾਰਡੀ ਬਹੁਤ ਹੀ ਨਿਰਾਸ਼ਾਵਾਦੀ (pessimistic) ਸੀ। ਉਸਦਾ ਮੰਨਣਾ ਸੀ ਕਿ ਇਨਸਾਨ ਦੀ ਕਿਸਮਤ ਪਹਿਲਾਂ ਹੀ ਤੈਅ ਹੈ, ਅਤੇ ਕੁਦਰਤ ਜਾਂ ਰੱਬ ਨੂੰ ਇਨਸਾਨ ਦੇ ਦੁੱਖਾਂ ਦੀ ਕੋਈ ਪਰਵਾਹ ਨਹੀਂ ਹੈ। ਉਸਦੇ ਨਾਵਲ ਇੱਕ ਖਾਸ ਇਲਾਕੇ ਵਿੱਚ ਸੈੱਟ ਹੁੰਦੇ ਹਨ ਜਿਸਨੂੰ ਉਸਨੇ 'Wessex' (ਵੈਸੈਕਸ) ਦਾ ਨਾਮ ਦਿੱਤਾ। ਉਸਦਾ ਸਭ ਤੋਂ ਮਸ਼ਹੂਰ ਅਤੇ ਦੁਖਦਾਈ ਨਾਵਲ Tess of the d'Urbervilles ਹੈ। ਉਸਦਾ ਆਖਰੀ ਨਾਵਲ Jude the Obscure ਇੰਨਾ ਜ਼ਿਆਦਾ ਨਿਰਾਸ਼ਾਵਾਦੀ ਸੀ ਕਿ ਲੋਕਾਂ ਨੇ ਉਸਦਾ ਵਿਰੋਧ ਕੀਤਾ, ਜਿਸ ਤੋਂ ਬਾਅਦ ਹਾਰਡੀ ਨੇ ਨਾਵਲ ਲਿਖਣੇ ਛੱਡ ਦਿੱਤੇ ਅਤੇ ਸਿਰਫ਼ ਕਵਿਤਾਵਾਂ ਲਿਖੀਆਂ।
