Later Romantics: Coleridge, Byron, Shelley, Keats, Austen

Supernaturalism • Hellenism • Novel of Manners

Samuel Taylor Coleridge

  • Collaborated with Wordsworth on Lyrical Ballads.
  • Focused on making the supernatural seem real.
  • Major poetry: The Rime of the Ancient Mariner, Kubla Khan.
  • Prose: Biographia Literaria (Coined 'Willing suspension of disbelief').

Byron and Shelley

Lord Byron

  • The 'Byronic Hero' (dark, brooding outcast).
  • Don Juan (Masterpiece, a satirical epic).
  • Massively popular, scandalous life.

P.B. Shelley

  • Radical political idealist and atheist.
  • Ode to the West Wind, Ozymandias.
  • Adonais (An elegy written for Keats).

John Keats

  • Died of tuberculosis at age 25.
  • Apolitical; devoted entirely to Beauty and art (Hellenism).
  • Wrote the 'Great Odes' of 1819 (e.g., Ode on a Grecian Urn).
  • Coined the term 'Negative Capability'.

Jane Austen

  • Wrote during the Romantic period but was NOT a Romantic.
  • Perfected the 'Novel of Manners'.
  • Focused on the social and economic realities of courtship among the landed gentry.
  • Key works: Pride and Prejudice, Emma.
  • Known for her brilliant, detached irony.

Key Elegy

Adonais

The famous pastoral elegy written by Percy Bysshe Shelley to mourn the tragic early death of John Keats.
