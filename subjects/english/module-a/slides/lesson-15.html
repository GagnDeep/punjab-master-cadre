Modern Age II: Yeats, Shaw, Beckett

Symbolism • Drama of Ideas • Theatre of the Absurd

W.B. Yeats

  • Key figure of the Irish Literary Revival.
  • Early work used heavy Symbolism and Irish myth; later work was prophetic and mystical.
  • The Second Coming: Prophesies a terrifying new historical era ('Things fall apart...').
  • Sailing to Byzantium: Explores aging and the immortality of art.

G.B. Shaw

  • Irish playwright, critic, and socialist.
  • Revolutionized the stage with the 'Drama of Ideas' (or Problem Play).
  • Replaced Victorian melodrama with intellectual debate and social satire.
  • Major works: Arms and the Man, Man and Superman.

Pygmalion

The Plot

  • Prof. Henry Higgins wagers he can pass off a cockney flower girl as a duchess.
  • He does this simply by teaching her proper phonetics.

The Meaning

  • A biting satire on the rigid British class system.
  • Shows how society judges people entirely on their accents.

Samuel Beckett

  • Avant-garde Irish writer.
  • Central figure of the 'Theatre of the Absurd'.
  • Plays reflect existential meaninglessness, featuring repetitive actions and no traditional plot.

Waiting for Godot

Absurdism

Beckett's masterpiece. Famously described as 'a play in which nothing happens, twice,' as Vladimir and Estragon wait endlessly.
