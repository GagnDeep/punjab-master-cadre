Early Romantics: Blake & Wordsworth

Pre-Romantics • Lyrical Ballads

The Pre-Romantics

  • Reacted against the strict, logical rules of the Augustan Age.
  • Reintroduced emotion, melancholy, and nature to poetry.
  • Thomas Gray: 'Elegy Written in a Country Churchyard'.
  • William Blake: Visionary poet ('Songs of Innocence & Experience').

The Spark of Romanticism

1798

The year 'Lyrical Ballads' was published by Wordsworth and Coleridge, officially marking the start of the English Romantic movement.

Classicism vs. Romanticism

Augustan Age

  • Reason & Intellect
  • Order & Restraint
  • Focus on High Society
  • Heroic Couplets

Romantic Age

  • Emotion & Imagination
  • Rebellion & Freedom
  • Focus on the Common Man
  • Lyrical, Free Forms

William Wordsworth

  • The supreme poet of Nature (Pantheism).
  • Defined poetry as 'the spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings'.
  • Major works: Tintern Abbey, The Daffodils.
  • The Prelude: His massive, autobiographical epic poem.

Key Quotes

  • 'The Child is father of the Man.' (Wordsworth, My Heart Leaps Up)
  • 'The paths of glory lead but to the grave.' (Gray, Elegy)
  • 'Tyger Tyger, burning bright...' (Blake, Songs of Experience)
