Modern Age I: T.S. Eliot, Woolf, Joyce

Stream of Consciousness • The Waste Land

The Modernist Era

  • Born from the trauma and disillusionment of World War I.
  • Rejection of linear, traditional Victorian narrative and poetic forms.
  • Key slogan: 'Make it New' (Ezra Pound).
  • Themes: Fragmentation, alienation, spiritual emptiness.

Stream of Consciousness

  • A revolutionary narrative technique.
  • Attempts to replicate the continuous, chaotic, unfiltered flow of a character's internal thoughts.
  • Heavily utilized by Virginia Woolf and James Joyce.

T.S. Eliot

The Waste Land (1922)

  • The seminal Modernist poem.
  • Highly fragmented and allusive.
  • Portrays Europe as a spiritual desert.
  • Opening: 'April is the cruellest month'.

Criticism

  • Coined the 'Objective Correlative'.
  • Argued that poetry should be impersonal.

Woolf & Joyce

Virginia Woolf

  • Mrs. Dalloway (1 day).
  • To the Lighthouse.
  • A Room of One's Own (Foundational feminist text).

James Joyce

  • Ulysses (1922) - chronicles 1 day in Dublin, mirroring Homer.
  • Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man.

The Miracle Year

1922

The landmark year of Modernism, seeing the publication of both Eliot's 'The Waste Land' and Joyce's 'Ulysses'.
