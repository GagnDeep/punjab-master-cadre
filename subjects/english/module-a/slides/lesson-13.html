Later Victorians: Dickens, Hardy, Eliot, Arnold

Social Realism • Pessimism • Criticism

Later Victorian Era

  • The Novel becomes the dominant literary form.
  • Focus shifts to Social Realism.
  • Authors tackle gritty realities: industrialization, poverty, class struggle, and institutional hypocrisy.

Charles Dickens

Style & Themes

  • Published in serial installments.
  • Fierce social critique (workhouses, courts).
  • Memorable, caricatured characters.
  • High sentimentality.

Major Novels

  • Great Expectations (Pip)
  • David Copperfield
  • Oliver Twist
  • A Tale of Two Cities

Thomas Hardy

  • Represents the pessimistic, darker end of the Victorian era.
  • Philosophy: Fatalism / Determinism (humans are victims of blind fate and indifferent nature).
  • Setting: The fictional, rural region of 'Wessex'.
  • Major Works: Tess of the d'Urbervilles, The Mayor of Casterbridge, Jude the Obscure.

George Eliot

  • Real name: Mary Ann Evans (used a male pseudonym to be taken seriously).
  • Pioneered intense psychological realism.
  • Masterpiece: Middlemarch ('A Study of Provincial Life').
  • Focus on the complex internal moral choices of her characters.

Dover Beach

Loss of Faith

Matthew Arnold's famous poem lamenting the 'withdrawing roar' of the Sea of Faith in the industrial age.
