Early Victorians: Tennyson, Browning, Brontës

Dramatic Monologue • Gothic Romance

The Victorian Age

  • 1837–1901: Reign of Queen Victoria.
  • Period of industrial expansion and Empire.
  • The 'Victorian Compromise': Tension between religion and science (Darwin), and public morality vs. private hypocrisy.

Alfred, Lord Tennyson

  • The voice of Victorian England; Poet Laureate for 42 years.
  • Poetry captures the era's melancholic doubt regarding faith and science.
  • In Memoriam A.H.H.: Massive elegy for his friend Arthur Henry Hallam.
  • Ulysses: Famous dramatic monologue ('To strive, to seek...').

Robert Browning

The Dramatic Monologue

  • A single speaker (not the poet) addresses a silent listener.
  • Inadvertently reveals dark psychological secrets/flaws.

Key Poems

  • My Last Duchess (A Duke admits to murder).
  • Porphyria's Lover (Strangling for love).

The Brontë Sisters

Charlotte Brontë

  • Wrote 'Jane Eyre' (1847).
  • Fiercely independent heroine.
  • Includes Gothic element of the 'madwoman in the attic'.

Emily Brontë

  • Wrote 'Wuthering Heights' (1847).
  • Her only novel.
  • Dark, violent tale of obsessive love (Heathcliff).
