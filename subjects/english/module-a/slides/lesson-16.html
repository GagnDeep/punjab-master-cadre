Post-War • Post-Colonial Literature

Post-War British Literature

  • Reaction against the complex, highly intellectual style of Modernism (like Eliot/Joyce).
  • Return to realism and colloquial language.
  • Philip Larkin & 'The Movement': Known for conversational, structured, highly pessimistic poetry about everyday English life.

George Orwell

Animal Farm (1945)

  • Allegorical fable.
  • Satirizes the Russian Revolution and Stalinism.
  • 'All animals are equal, but some... are more equal.'

1984 (1949)

  • Dystopian novel.
  • Critique of totalitarianism.
  • Coined 'Big Brother' and 'Thought Police'.

William Golding

  • World War II experiences convinced him of human depravity.
  • Masterpiece: Lord of the Flies (1954).
  • Plot: Stranded British schoolboys descend into savagery and murder.
  • Theme: The inherent evil and darkness present in all human nature.

Post-Colonial Voices

Salman Rushdie

  • Master of 'Magic Realism'.
  • Midnight's Children (1981).
  • Links telepathic children to the birth of independent India.

V.S. Naipaul & Heaney

  • Naipaul: Explored displacement and identity (A House for Mr Biswas).
  • Heaney: Irish Nobel Laureate focused on rural landscapes and history.
