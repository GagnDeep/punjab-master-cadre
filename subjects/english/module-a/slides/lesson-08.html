Augustan Age: Pope & Swift

Neo-Classicism • Prose & Satire

The Augustan Age (Neoclassicism)

  • Early 18th century. Also known as the 'Age of Reason'.
  • Emulated Roman classical ideals: order, balance, and logic.
  • Emotion and imagination were suppressed in favor of intellect.
  • The Heroic Couplet was the dominant poetic form.

Alexander Pope

  • The greatest poet of the 18th century.
  • Mastered the Heroic Couplet to absolute technical perfection.
  • Famous works: An Essay on Criticism, The Dunciad.
  • Most famous quotes: 'A little learning is a dangerous thing', 'To err is human, to forgive, divine'.

The Rape of the Lock

Genre: Mock-Epic

  • Treats a trivial subject with grand, epic style.
  • Satirizes the vanity of high society.
  • Features epic battles and supernatural 'sylphs'.

The Plot

  • A Lord cuts a lock of hair from Arabella Fermor's head.
  • Causes a massive, ridiculous social feud.

Jonathan Swift

  • The greatest prose satirist in the English language.
  • Wrote dark, misanthropic satires on human nature and politics.
  • Gulliver's Travels: A multi-layered satire, not just a children's book.
  • The Battle of the Books: Ancients vs. Moderns.

A Modest Proposal

Irony

Swift's essay 'suggesting' the poor sell their children as food is a masterpiece of savage irony targeting British oppression in Ireland.
