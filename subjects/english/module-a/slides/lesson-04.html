Jacobean Age & John Donne

Metaphysical Poetry • Conceits

The Jacobean Era

  • 1603–1625: Reign of King James I.
  • A shift to a darker, more questioning literary tone.
  • 1611: Publication of the King James Bible.
  • Rise of Metaphysical and Cavalier poets.

Metaphysical Poetry

  • Term coined by critic Samuel Johnson.
  • Blends intense emotion with complex intellect and logic.
  • Abrupt, dramatic openings (in media res).
  • Heavy use of paradox and irony.

The Metaphysical Conceit

  • The defining feature of this poetry school.
  • An extended, highly unusual metaphor.
  • Compares two fundamentally different things.
  • Example: Comparing lovers' souls to a drafting compass.

John Donne's Major Works

  • A Valediction: Forbidding Mourning (The Compass conceit).
  • The Flea (Erotic, logical argument using an insect).
  • Holy Sonnets (Includes 'Death be not proud').
  • Meditation XVII ('No man is an island').
