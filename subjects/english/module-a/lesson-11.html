11. Later Romantics: Coleridge, Byron, Shelley, Keats, Austen

ਬਾਅਦ ਦੇ ਰੋਮਾਂਟਿਕਸ

Why this matters (ERB Pattern)

Identify who wrote which famous Elegy (Shelley wrote 'Adonais' for Keats). Also, matching literary concepts to their authors (Coleridge = 'Willing suspension of disbelief', Keats = 'Negative Capability').

ਪ੍ਰੀਖਿਆ ਵਿੱਚ 'Adonais' ਕਿਸਨੇ ਕਿਸ ਲਈ ਲਿਖਿਆ ਅਤੇ 'Negative Capability' ਸ਼ਬਦ ਕਿਸਨੇ ਦਿੱਤਾ, ਇਹ ਪੁੱਛਿਆ ਜਾਂਦਾ ਹੈ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Analyze Coleridge's supernatural elements and critical concepts.
  • Distinguish the unique styles of the 2nd generation: Byron (satire/rebellion), Shelley (idealism), Keats (beauty/senses).
  • Understand Jane Austen's creation of the 'Novel of Manners' outside the Romantic mainstream.

1. Samuel Taylor Coleridge (1772–1834)

S.T. Coleridge was Wordsworth's closest friend and collaborator on Lyrical Ballads. While Wordsworth focused on making the ordinary seem extraordinary, Coleridge's goal was to make the extraordinary and supernatural seem real. He was a profound philosopher and critic, but struggled with opium addiction, which limited his poetic output.

  • The Rime of the Ancient Mariner: His masterpiece, opening Lyrical Ballads. It is a haunting, supernatural ballad about a sailor who is cursed after shooting an albatross. (Famous line: "Water, water, every where, / Nor any drop to drink.")
  • Kubla Khan: A dream-vision poem written under the influence of opium. It describes a mystical pleasure-dome in Xanadu. It was famously interrupted by a "person from Porlock" and remains unfinished.
  • Christabel: A gothic, unfinished poem about a pure maiden and an evil enchantress.
  • Biographia Literaria (1817): His major prose work of literary criticism, famous for defining the concepts of "Fancy" vs. "Imagination" and introducing the phrase "willing suspension of disbelief."

2. The Second Generation of Romantics

While Wordsworth and Coleridge grew older and politically conservative, a younger, more radical trio of poets emerged.

Lord Byron (1788–1824)

Byron was the literary rockstar of his age—scandalous, wildly popular, and rebellious. He created the archetype of the Byronic Hero: a dark, brooding, passionate, and rebellious outcast carrying a secret guilt.

  • Childe Harold's Pilgrimage: Made him famous overnight. It follows a melancholic young man traveling across Europe.
  • Don Juan: His masterpiece. A massive, unfinished satirical epic. Byron reverses the legend, making Don Juan not a heartless seducer, but an innocent young man who is constantly seduced by women.

Percy Bysshe Shelley (1792–1822)

Shelley was a radical idealist, atheist, and political revolutionary. He believed in the ultimate perfectibility of mankind and that poets were "the unacknowledged legislators of the world."

  • Ode to the West Wind: A powerful poem asking the wind to spread his revolutionary ideas across the world ("If Winter comes, can Spring be far behind?").
  • Ozymandias: A famous sonnet about the inevitable decay of tyrannical power (describing a shattered statue in the desert).
  • Adonais: A beautiful pastoral elegy written in Spenserian stanzas to mourn the death of John Keats.

John Keats (1795–1821)

Keats died of tuberculosis at age 25, yet produced some of the greatest poetry in English. Unlike Byron and Shelley, he was not interested in politics or rebellion, but in pure beauty and sensory experience (Hellenism). He coined the concept of "Negative Capability": the ability to remain in doubts and uncertainties without reaching after fact or reason.

  • The Great Odes (1819): His crowning achievement, including Ode to a Nightingale and Ode on a Grecian Urn (Famous line: "Beauty is truth, truth beauty").
  • Endymion: (Famous opening: "A thing of beauty is a joy for ever").

3. Jane Austen (1775–1817)

While the Romantic poets explored extreme emotion and supernatural realms, Jane Austen wrote quietly in the countryside. She perfected the Novel of Manners, focusing on the social lives, courtships, and economic realities of the English landed gentry. Her writing is characterized by brilliant irony, psychological realism, and sharp social commentary, operating entirely outside the Romantic mainstream.

Key works: Pride and Prejudice, Sense and Sensibility, Emma.

Worked Example

Q: The critical concept of 'willing suspension of disbelief' was coined by which Romantic writer in his Biographia Literaria?

Step 1: Recall the phrase 'willing suspension of disbelief'.

Step 2: Identify it as a concept of literary criticism.

Step 3: Match it to the major critical work of the era, 'Biographia Literaria'.

Step 4: Select Samuel Taylor Coleridge.

🧠 How ERB Asks It

Memorize the Elegies! Milton's 'Lycidas' is for Edward King. Shelley's 'Adonais' is for John Keats. Tennyson's 'In Memoriam' is for A.H.H. (We will cover Tennyson next).

Quick Recap

  • Coleridge: The Ancient Mariner (Supernatural). Coined 'Willing suspension of disbelief' and 'Fancy vs. Imagination'.
  • Byron: Don Juan (Satire). Created the brooding 'Byronic Hero'.
  • Shelley: Ode to the West Wind. Wrote 'Adonais' (Elegy for Keats). Radical idealist.
  • Keats: The Great Odes. Obsessed with Beauty. Coined 'Negative Capability'. Died at 25.
  • Jane Austen: Novel of Manners (Pride and Prejudice). Realistic, ironic, focused on landed gentry marriage/money.
