Ocean Currents

  • Ocean currents are continuous, directed movements of ocean water generated by wind, the Coriolis effect, and differences in water density.
  • Warm currents flow from the equator towards the poles (e.g., Gulf Stream, Kuroshio)

Visualizing the Concept

Tides

Tides are the regular rise and fall of sea levels caused by the gravitational forces exerted by the Moon, Sun, and Earth's rotation. Spring Tides: Occur during full and new moons when the Sun, Earth, and Moon are aligned, causing the highest high tides. Neap Tides: Occur during quarter moons when the Sun and Moon are at right angles, causing lower high tides.

Key Point

Always remember the distinctions between the major categories discussed here.

Be prepared to match currents with their oceans (e.g., Agulhas = Indian Ocean, Kuroshio = Pacific Ocean).

Major Oceans

  • Pacific: Largest, deepest. Ring of Fire.
  • Atlantic: S-shaped, busiest trade routes.
  • Indian: Only ocean named after a country.
  • Arctic & Southern: Polar oceans.

Ocean Currents

  • Continuous, directed movements of ocean water.
  • Driven by wind, Coriolis effect, temperature, and salinity differences.
  • Warm Currents: Flow from equator to poles (e.g., Gulf Stream).
  • Cold Currents: Flow from poles to equator (e.g., Labrador Current).

Importance of Currents

  • They regulate global climate by distributing heat.
  • Meeting of warm and cold currents (e.g., near Newfoundland or Japan) creates thick fog but excellent fishing grounds.

Water Distribution

97%

of Earth's water is salty ocean water. Only 3% is fresh.

Tides

  • Rise and fall of sea levels caused by gravitational pull of Moon and Sun.
  • Spring Tides: Highest tides. Sun, Moon, and Earth align (Full/New Moon).
  • Neap Tides: Lower tides. Sun and Moon are at right angles.

Quick Recap

  • Warm currents flow poleward; cold currents flow equatorward.
  • Tides are driven by the Moon and Sun's gravity.
  • Spring tides are extreme; Neap tides are moderate.
