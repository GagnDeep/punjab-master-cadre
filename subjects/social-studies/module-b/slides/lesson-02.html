Pressure Belts and Winds

Differences in solar heating create global pressure belts: Equatorial Low, Subtropical High, Subpolar Low, and Polar High. Winds move from high to low pressure. The Coriolis Effect deflects winds to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and left in the Southern Hemisphere. Major planetary winds include Trade Winds (blowing towards the equator), Westerlies, and Polar Easterlies.

Key Point

Always remember the distinctions between the major categories discussed here.

Questions often confuse the Stratosphere (Ozone) and Ionosphere (Radio waves). Make sure you clearly distinguish their functions.
- ERB Exam Pattern Tip

Atmospheric Layers (1-2)

  • Troposphere: Weather occurs here. Temperature drops with altitude.
  • Stratosphere: Contains the Ozone layer. Planes fly here. Temperature rises.

Atmospheric Layers (3-5)

  • Mesosphere: Coldest layer. Meteors burn up here.
  • Thermosphere: Contains Ionosphere (reflects radio waves). Aurora borealis occurs here.
  • Exosphere: Outermost limit.

Pressure Belts

  • Equatorial Low (Doldrums): Intense heat causes air to rise.
  • Subtropical High (Horse Latitudes): Sinking air creates high pressure.
  • Subpolar Low & Polar High.

Winds

  • Winds move from High Pressure to Low Pressure.
  • Coriolis Effect: Earth's rotation deflects winds right in the North, left in the South.
  • Trade Winds: Blow from Subtropical High to Equatorial Low. Reliable for sailing.

Quick Recap

  • Atmosphere layers: Troposphere, Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Thermosphere.
  • Pressure belts are created by uneven heating.
  • Winds are deflected by the Coriolis effect.
