Climate and Monsoons

  • India's climate is of the 'monsoon' type.
  • The South-West Monsoon (June-Sept) brings the majority of rainfall to the country, striking the Malabar coast first

Soils and Agriculture

1. Alluvial Soil: Most widespread, highly fertile (wheat, rice). Found in northern plains. 2. Black Soil (Regur): Ideal for cotton, found in the Deccan trap region. Retains moisture. 3. Laterite Soil: Leached soils found in heavy rainfall areas, good for tea and cashew nuts. Major crop seasons are Kharif (sown in June, harvested in Sept/Oct - e.g., Rice, Cotton) and Rabi (sown in Oct/Nov, harvested in April - e.g., Wheat, Mustard).

Key Point

Always remember the distinctions between the major categories discussed here.

The Monsoon Mechanism

  • Driven by differential heating of land and water.
  • Summer: Land is hot (Low Pressure), Sea is cool (High Pressure). Winds blow Sea to Land (SW Monsoon).
  • Winter: Land is cool (High Pressure), Sea is warm (Low Pressure). Winds blow Land to Sea (NE Monsoon).

Winter Rainfall

  • Western Disturbances: Cyclonic storms from the Mediterranean bring winter rain to Punjab/Haryana. Crucial for Rabi wheat.
  • Retreating Monsoon: Brings winter rain to the Coromandel Coast (Tamil Nadu).

Major Soils

  • Alluvial: Most fertile, river valleys (Wheat, Rice).
  • Black (Regur): Deccan plateau, retains moisture (Cotton).
  • Laterite: Heavy rain areas, intense leaching (Tea, Cashew).
  • Red & Yellow: Iron-rich, less fertile without fertilizers.

Cropping Seasons

  • Kharif: Sown with monsoon (June-July), harvested Sept-Oct. (Rice, Maize, Cotton).
  • Rabi: Sown in winter (Oct-Dec), harvested April-May. (Wheat, Gram, Mustard).
  • Zaid: Short summer season (Watermelon, Cucumber).

Quick Recap

  • SW Monsoon brings summer rain; NE Monsoon brings winter rain to Tamil Nadu.
  • Black soil = Cotton; Laterite soil = Tea/Coffee.
  • Kharif crops are monsoon-dependent; Rabi crops are sown in winter.
