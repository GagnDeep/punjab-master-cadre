Drainage Systems

1. Himalayan Rivers: Perennial (water all year), long courses. Key systems: Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra. 2. Peninsular Rivers: Seasonal, shorter courses. Key rivers: Narmada and Tapi (West flowing, form estuaries), Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri (East flowing, form deltas).

Key Point

Always remember the distinctions between the major categories discussed here.

Questions often focus on exceptions: Narmada and Tapi flow west and do not form deltas, unlike most other major peninsular rivers.

Physiographic Divisions

  • The Himalayas (Young fold mountains)
  • The Northern Plains (Formed by alluvium)
  • The Peninsular Plateau (Oldest landmass, igneous/metamorphic rocks)
  • The Indian Desert, Coastal Plains, and Islands.

The Himalayas

  • Himadri (Greater Himalayas): Highest, snow-bound (e.g., Everest, Kanchenjunga).
  • Himachal (Middle Himalayas): Hill stations (Shimla, Darjeeling).
  • Shiwaliks (Outer Himalayas): Lowest, youngest range.

Himalayan Rivers

  • Perennial: Flow all year (snowmelt + rain).
  • Indus System: Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej.
  • Ganga System: Yamuna, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi.
  • Brahmaputra System: Known as Tsangpo in Tibet.

Peninsular Rivers

  • Seasonal: Depend on monsoon rain.
  • East-flowing: Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri. Form Deltas.
  • West-flowing: Narmada, Tapi. Form Estuaries (flow in rift valleys).

Quick Recap

  • India has 5 major physical divisions.
  • Himalayan rivers are perennial; Peninsular are seasonal.
  • Narmada and Tapi are west-flowing estuary builders.
