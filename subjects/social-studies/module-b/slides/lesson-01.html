Earth's Interior

  • The Earth is divided into three main layers: Crust, Mantle, and Core.
  • The Crust is the outermost solid shell

Visualizing the Concept

Types of Rocks

1. Igneous Rocks: Formed by the cooling of magma (e.g., Granite, Basalt). 2. Sedimentary Rocks: Formed by the accumulation of sediments over time (e.g., Sandstone, Limestone). They often contain fossils. 3. Metamorphic Rocks: Formed when existing rocks are subjected to high heat and pressure (e.g., Marble from Limestone).

Key Point

Always remember the distinctions between the major categories discussed here.

ERB frequently asks about the 'discontinuities' inside the Earth. Remember: Moho = Crust/Mantle, Gutenberg = Mantle/Core.
- ERB Exam Pattern Tip

Layers of the Earth (Details)

  • Crust: Outermost layer. Solid. 5-70km thick. Divided into continental (SiAl) and oceanic (SiMa).
  • Mantle: Thickest layer (~2900km). Contains magma in the asthenosphere.
  • Core: Innermost. Outer core is liquid (generates magnetic field), inner core is solid. (NiFe).

Discontinuities

  • These are boundaries between layers where seismic waves change speed.
  • Moho: Between Crust and Mantle.
  • Gutenberg: Between Mantle and Core.

Rock Types

  • Igneous: Primary rocks. Formed from cooling magma (e.g., Granite, Basalt).
  • Sedimentary: Formed from accumulation and compaction of sediments. Contains fossils (e.g., Sandstone).
  • Metamorphic: Existing rocks changed by heat and pressure (e.g., Marble from Limestone).

Earth's Volume

84%

of the Earth's volume is made up by the Mantle.

Landforms

  • Endogenic Forces: Internal forces like volcanism and earthquakes. Create mountains and plateaus.
  • Exogenic Forces: External forces like wind, water, and ice. Erode mountains and create valleys and plains.

Quick Recap

  • Earth consists of Crust, Mantle, and Core.
  • Rocks are Igneous, Sedimentary, and Metamorphic.
  • Landforms are shaped by internal (tectonic) and external (erosion) forces.
