Minerals

  • Minerals are natural substances with specific chemical compositions.
  • Metallic minerals yield metals (e.g., Iron ore, Bauxite, Copper)

Visualizing the Concept

Energy Resources

Conventional Energy: Fossil fuels like Coal, Petroleum, and Natural Gas. They are exhaustible and cause pollution. Coal is India's primary commercial energy source (Gondwana coalfields). Non-Conventional Energy: Renewable sources like Solar, Wind, Biogas, and Tidal energy. They are sustainable. India is expanding solar power rapidly (e.g., Bhadla Solar Park).

Key Point

Always remember the distinctions between the major categories discussed here.

Questions often pair a mineral with its largest producing state. Create a cheat sheet: Iron/Bauxite -> Odisha, Copper -> MP/Rajasthan, Gold -> Karnataka.

Types of Minerals

  • Metallic: Yield metals. Ferrous (contain iron - Iron Ore, Manganese) and Non-ferrous (Copper, Bauxite).
  • Non-metallic: Do not yield metals (Mica, Salt, Limestone).
  • Energy Minerals: Coal, Petroleum, Natural Gas.

Key Producing States

  • Iron Ore & Bauxite: Odisha is the leading producer.
  • Copper: Madhya Pradesh (Balaghat) and Rajasthan (Khetri).
  • Gold: Karnataka (Kolar).
  • Mica: Andhra Pradesh and Jharkhand.

Energy Resources

  • Conventional: Fossil fuels (Coal, Oil). Exhaustible, polluting. Coal is India's primary energy source.
  • Non-Conventional: Renewable (Solar, Wind, Biomass). Sustainable, clean.

Conservation & Sustainability

  • Minerals are finite and take millions of years to form.
  • Sustainable Development: Meeting present needs without compromising future generations.
  • Strategies: Use renewable energy, recycle metals, improve mining technology.

Quick Recap

  • Minerals are metallic (Iron, Bauxite) or non-metallic (Mica, Coal).
  • Energy transitions from conventional (Coal) to non-conventional (Solar, Wind).
  • Conservation ensures resources for future generations (Sustainable Development).
