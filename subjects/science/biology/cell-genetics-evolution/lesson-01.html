Cell Division: Mitosis & Meiosis

ਸੈੱਲ ਵੰਡ: ਮਾਈਟੋਸਿਸ ਅਤੇ ਮਿਓਸਿਸ (Cell Division)

Why this matters (ERB 2021)

A direct question was asked about the specific stage where crossing over occurs (Pachytene). Identifying the key events of Prophase I in Meiosis is a high-yield topic that examiners love.

ਮਿਓਸਿਸ ਦੀਆਂ ਵੱਖ-ਵੱਖ ਸਟੇਜਾਂ 'ਤੇ ਪੇਪਰ ਵਿੱਚ ਸਿੱਧਾ ਸਵਾਲ ਆਉਂਦਾ ਹੈ (ਖਾਸ ਕਰਕੇ Prophase I)।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Understand the phases of the cell cycle ($G_1, S, G_2, M$).
  • Distinguish between the outcomes of Mitosis and Meiosis.
  • Memorize the 5 sub-stages of Prophase I in Meiosis and their defining events.

1. The Cell Cycle

The cell cycle consists of Interphase (prep phase) and M-phase (division phase).

  • $G_1$ Phase: Cell grows, organelles duplicate.
  • $S$ Phase (Synthesis): DNA replication occurs. The amount of DNA doubles (2C to 4C), but the chromosome number remains the same (2n).
  • $G_2$ Phase: Proteins are synthesized in preparation for mitosis.
  • $M$ Phase: Mitosis or Meiosis occurs here.

2. Mitosis vs Meiosis

Mitosis happens in somatic (body) cells. It's an equational division. 1 parent cell (2n) $\rightarrow$ 2 identical daughter cells (2n).

Meiosis happens in germ cells (to make sperm/egg). It's a reductional division. 1 parent cell (2n) $\rightarrow$ 4 non-identical daughter cells (n).

The Crucial Stages of Prophase I (Meiosis)

This is where genetic variation is created. Memorize the mnemonic: Little Zara Plays During Day.

  • Leptotene: Chromosomes become visible.
  • Zygotene: Homologous chromosomes pair up (Synapsis). Bivalent/Tetrad forms.
  • Pachytene: Crossing over occurs between non-sister chromatids. Recombination nodule forms.
  • Diplotene: Synaptonemal complex dissolves. X-shaped structures called Chiasmata become visible.
  • Diakinesis: Terminalization of chiasmata. Nuclear envelope breaks down.

Worked Example: DNA Content

Q: If the initial amount of DNA in a cell in $G_1$ is 20 picograms (pg) and it has 10 chromosomes. What will be the DNA content and chromosome number at the end of the $S$ phase?

Step 1: Recall the definition of S phase: DNA replicates, but centromeres do not divide.

Step 2: DNA content doubles. $20 \text{ pg} \times 2 = 40 \text{ pg}$.

Step 3: Chromosome number remains the same because the sister chromatids are still attached at the same centromere.

Answer: DNA content = 40 pg, Chromosomes = 10.

🧠 How ERB Asks It

They love testing Anaphase I vs Anaphase II.
In Anaphase I of meiosis, homologous chromosomes separate (centromeres DO NOT split).
In Anaphase II (and Anaphase of Mitosis), sister chromatids separate (centromeres DO split).

Quick Recap

  • S phase: DNA doubles (2C $\rightarrow$ 4C), but chromosome number is constant.
  • Zygotene = Synapsis.
  • Pachytene = Crossing over.
  • Diplotene = Chiasmata visible.
  • Meiosis Anaphase I: Centromeres don't split.
