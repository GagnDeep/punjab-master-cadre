Work, Energy and Collisions

ਕੰਮ, ਊਰਜਾ ਅਤੇ ਟਕਰਾਅ (Collisions)

Why this matters (ERB Pattern)

Problems involving conservative forces, calculating potential energy from force functions, and finding final velocities in inelastic collisions are heavily tested. You need to identify when to conserve momentum versus energy instantly.

ਰੂੜੀਵਾਦੀ ਬਲਾਂ (conservative forces) ਅਤੇ ਟਕਰਾਅ ਦੇ ਸਵਾਲ ਅਕਸਰ ਆਉਂਦੇ ਹਨ। ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਤੁਰੰਤ ਪਛਾਣਨਾ ਪਵੇਗਾ ਕਿ ਕਦੋਂ ਮੋਮੈਂਟਮ ਬਚਾਉਣਾ ਹੈ ਅਤੇ ਕਦੋਂ ਊਰਜਾ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Differentiate between conservative and non-conservative forces.
  • Calculate potential energy \( U(x) \) from a given force function \( F(x) \).
  • Apply the coefficient of restitution \( e \) to solve 1D collision problems.

1. Conservative Forces & Potential Energy

A force is conservative if the work done by it over a closed path is zero (e.g., gravity, spring force). For a conservative force, we can define a potential energy function \( U \):

$$ \vec{F} = -\nabla U $$

In 1D: \( F(x) = -\frac{dU}{dx} \)

This means the force always acts in the direction that minimizes potential energy.

Common Trap

Forgetting the negative sign! If \( U(x) = cx^2 \), then \( F(x) = -2cx \), meaning the force is attractive (restoring).

2. Collisions & Coefficient of Restitution

In all collisions (in absence of external forces), momentum is conserved.

The type of collision is determined by the coefficient of restitution (\( e \)):

$$ e = \frac{\text{Velocity of Separation}}{\text{Velocity of Approach}} = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{u_1 - u_2} $$

  • Elastic Collision: \( e = 1 \). Both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved.
  • Inelastic Collision: \( 0 < e < 1 \). Kinetic energy is lost.
  • Perfectly Inelastic: \( e = 0 \). Objects stick together (\( v_1 = v_2 \)). Maximum KE loss.

Worked Example: Perfectly Inelastic Collision

Q: A bullet of mass \( m \) moving with velocity \( v \) strikes a stationary wooden block of mass \( M \) and gets embedded in it. What is the final velocity of the system?

Step 1: Identify the collision. "Embedded" means perfectly inelastic (\( e = 0 \)).

Step 2: Conserve momentum. Initial momentum = Final momentum.

Step 3: \( m \cdot v + M \cdot 0 = (m + M) \cdot V_{\text{final}} \)

Answer: \( V_{\text{final}} = \frac{mv}{m + M} \)

🧠 How ERB Asks It

ERB rarely asks for just final velocity. They usually ask for the fraction of kinetic energy lost in a perfectly inelastic collision.
Memorize the shortcut: \( \text{Fraction Lost} = \frac{M}{m+M} \) (where M is the target mass).

Quick Recap

  • \( F = -\frac{dU}{dx} \). The derivative of potential energy gives the negative of the force.
  • Momentum is ALWAYS conserved in collisions (if \( F_{\text{ext}} = 0 \)).
  • Elastic: \( e = 1 \), KE conserved. Perfectly Inelastic: \( e = 0 \), bodies stick together.
