Electronic Configuration Rules

ਇਲੈਕਟ੍ਰਾਨਿਕ ਸੰਰਚਨਾ ਦੇ ਨਿਯਮ (Electronic Configuration Rules)

Why this matters (ERB Standard Pattern)

ERB almost always asks for the configuration of Chromium (Cr, Z=24) or Copper (Cu, Z=29) because they are exceptions to the standard rules. Knowing WHY they are exceptions (exchange energy and symmetry) is crucial.

ਪੇਪਰ ਵਿੱਚ ਕ੍ਰੋਮੀਅਮ (Cr) ਜਾਂ ਕਾਪਰ (Cu) ਦੀ ਇਲੈਕਟ੍ਰਾਨਿਕ ਸੰਰਚਨਾ ਜ਼ਰੂਰ ਪੁੱਛੀ ਜਾਂਦੀ ਹੈ ਕਿਉਂਕਿ ਇਹ ਆਮ ਨਿਯਮਾਂ ਨੂੰ ਤੋੜਦੇ ਹਨ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • State and apply the Aufbau Principle using the (n+l) rule.
  • Understand Pauli's Exclusion Principle.
  • Use Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity to fill degenerate orbitals.
  • Explain the extra stability of half-filled and fully-filled orbitals (Cr and Cu).

1. The Three Rules of Filling Orbitals

Aufbau Principle & The (n+l) Rule

Electrons fill the lowest energy orbitals first. To compare energy, use the (n + l) rule:

  • The orbital with the lower (n+l) value has lower energy.
  • If (n+l) is the same, the orbital with the lower 'n' has lower energy.

Example: 4s (n=4, l=0) $\implies$ n+l=4. 3d (n=3, l=2) $\implies$ n+l=5. Therefore, 4s fills before 3d.

Pauli Exclusion Principle

No two electrons in an atom can have the same four quantum numbers. This means an orbital can hold a maximum of 2 electrons, and they must have opposite spins.

Hund's Rule

For degenerate orbitals (orbitals with the same energy, like the three 2p orbitals), electron pairing will not occur until each orbital is singly occupied with parallel spins.

2. Exceptions: Half-Filled and Fully-Filled Stability

Orbitals that are exactly half-filled (e.g., $p^3, d^5, f^7$) or fully-filled (e.g., $p^6, d^{10}, f^{14}$) are unusually stable due to:

  • Symmetry: Symmetrical distribution of electron cloud shields the nucleus better.
  • Exchange Energy: Electrons with parallel spins can exchange positions. More exchanges mean lower energy and higher stability.

Worked Example: Copper (Z=29)

Q: Write the electronic configuration of Copper (Cu, Atomic Number 29).

Step 1: Follow Aufbau up to Argon (18 electrons): $[Ar]$. That leaves 11 electrons.

Step 2: Next are 4s and 3d. Normally, we would write: $4s^2 \, 3d^9$.

Step 3: Notice $3d^9$. It is one electron away from a stable fully-filled $d^{10}$ configuration.

Step 4: An electron shifts from 4s to 3d to gain this stability.

Answer: The correct configuration is $[Ar] \, 4s^1 \, 3d^{10}$.

🧠 How ERB Asks It

ERB will ask for the configuration of an ion of an exception element, like $Cu^{2+}$ or $Fe^{3+}$.
Crucial Rule: When removing electrons to form cations, ALWAYS remove from the outermost shell (highest 'n') first. For transition metals, remove from 4s BEFORE 3d.

Quick Recap

  • Aufbau: Lowest (n+l) fills first.
  • Pauli: Max 2 electrons per orbital, opposite spins.
  • Hund's: Singly occupy degenerate orbitals first.
  • Cr (24): $[Ar] \, 4s^1 \, 3d^5$
  • Cu (29): $[Ar] \, 4s^1 \, 3d^{10}$
