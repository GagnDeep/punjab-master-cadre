Quantum Numbers & Orbitals

ਕੁਆਂਟਮ ਨੰਬਰ ਅਤੇ ਔਰਬਿਟਲ (Orbitals)

Why this matters (ERB 2022)

Identifying the valid set of quantum numbers for an electron is a guaranteed +1 mark. If you know the rules for \( n, l, m, \) and \( s \), you can spot the impossible option in 10 seconds.

ਇਲੈਕਟ੍ਰੋਨ ਲਈ ਕੁਆਂਟਮ ਨੰਬਰਾਂ ਦੇ ਸਹੀ ਸੈੱਟ ਦੀ ਪਛਾਣ ਕਰਨਾ ਇੱਕ ਪੱਕਾ ਸਵਾਲ ਹੈ। ਜੇਕਰ ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਨਿਯਮ ਪਤਾ ਹਨ, ਤਾਂ ਤੁਸੀਂ 10 ਸਕਿੰਟਾਂ ਵਿੱਚ ਗਲਤ ਆਪਸ਼ਨ ਲੱਭ ਸਕਦੇ ਹੋ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Understand the physical meaning of the four quantum numbers.
  • Memorize the allowed values for each quantum number.
  • Identify nodes (radial and angular) in orbitals.

1. The Four Quantum Numbers

Quantum numbers are like the "postal address" of an electron in an atom.

  • Principal (\( n \)): Shell / Size / Energy. \( n = 1, 2, 3, \dots \)
  • Azimuthal (\( l \)): Subshell / Shape. \( l = 0, 1, 2, \dots, (n-1) \).
    Remember: \( l=0 \) (s), \( l=1 \) (p), \( l=2 \) (d), \( l=3 \) (f).
  • Magnetic (\( m_l \)): Orbital / Orientation. \( m_l = -l, \dots, 0, \dots, +l \).
    Total orbitals in a subshell = \( 2l + 1 \).
  • Spin (\( m_s \)): Electron spin. \( m_s = +\frac{1}{2}, -\frac{1}{2} \).
Common Trap

The azimuthal number \( l \) can never be equal to or greater than \( n \). E.g., \( 2d \) is impossible because if \( n=2 \), max \( l=1 \).

2. Nodes in Orbitals

A node is a region where the probability of finding an electron is exactly zero.

  • Angular Nodes: Determined by shape. Number of angular nodes = \( l \).
  • Radial Nodes: Determined by distance from nucleus. Number of radial nodes = \( n - l - 1 \).
  • Total Nodes: \( (n - l - 1) + l = n - 1 \).

Worked Example: Invalid Sets

Q: Which of the following sets of quantum numbers is NOT possible?

  1. \( n=3, l=2, m_l=-2, m_s=+\frac{1}{2} \)
  2. \( n=4, l=0, m_l=0, m_s=-\frac{1}{2} \)
  3. \( n=3, l=3, m_l=-1, m_s=+\frac{1}{2} \)
  4. \( n=5, l=1, m_l=0, m_s=-\frac{1}{2} \)

Step 1: Check if \( l < n \) for all options.

In option C, \( n=3 \) and \( l=3 \). This violates the rule \( l \leq n-1 \).

Answer: Option C is not possible. (It would represent a \( 3f \) orbital, which doesn't exist).

🧠 How ERB Asks It

ERB loves testing the \( m_l \) boundary. They will give you \( n=2, l=1 \), and then give an option with \( m_l = -2 \). Since \( m_l \) is bound by \( -l \) to \( +l \), for \( l=1 \), \( m_l \) can only be -1, 0, +1. Spot the out-of-bounds \( m_l \) quickly!

Quick Recap

  • \( n \): Shell (1,2,3...)
  • \( l \): Subshell (0=s, 1=p, 2=d, 3=f). Must be \( < n \).
  • \( m_l \): Orientation. Between \( -l \) and \( +l \).
  • Total nodes = \( n - 1 \). Angular nodes = \( l \).
