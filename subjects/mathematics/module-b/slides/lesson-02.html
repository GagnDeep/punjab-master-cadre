Module B • Lesson 02

Differentiability & Derivative Rules

Objectives

  • Understand the geometric meaning of differentiability.
  • Apply the Product and Quotient rules.
  • Master the Chain Rule for composite functions.
  • Memorize standard derivatives.

Differentiability

A function is differentiable if it has a defined, non-vertical tangent.

Rule of Thumb: The graph must be "smooth".

Non-Differentiable Points

  • Sharp corners (e.g., |x| at x=0)
  • Cusps
  • Vertical tangents
  • Discontinuities

The Golden Rule

Differentiability implies Continuity.

Continuity ≠ Differentiability

Product Rule

(uv)' = u'v + uv'

"Derivative of the first times the second, plus the first times derivative of the second."

Quotient Rule

(u/v)' = (u'v - uv') / v2

"Low d-high minus high d-low, square the bottom..."

The Chain Rule

For composite functions: f(g(x))

  • Differentiate the "outside" function f(u).
  • Leave the "inside" function g(x) alone.
  • Multiply by the derivative of the "inside" function g'(x).
  • Result: f'(g(x)) * g'(x)

Worked Example

Q: Derivative of y = sin(x2 + 3x)

Steps:

  • Outer function: sin(u) → derivative is cos(u)
  • Inner function: u = x2 + 3x → derivative is 2x + 3
  • Multiply: cos(x2 + 3x) * (2x + 3)

Solution

(2x + 3)cos(x2 + 3x)

Exam Pattern

Expect combinations of rules. E.g., Product rule where one factor needs the Chain rule.

Strategy

Keep your work organized. Write out 'u' and 'v' explicitly before applying formulas to avoid sign errors.

Quick Review

  • Smooth curve = Differentiable.
  • (uv)' = u'v + uv'
  • (u/v)' = (u'v - uv')/v2
  • Chain Rule: Outer deriv * Inner deriv.
