Lesson 10: Linear Inequalities & Mathematical Reasoning

ਰੇਖਿਕ ਅਸਮਾਨਤਾਵਾਂ ਅਤੇ ਗਣਿਤਿਕ ਤਰਕ

Why this matters (ERB Pattern)

Graphical solutions to inequalities and questions testing the contrapositive of a statement are staple questions. These are pure logic and can be solved very quickly.

ਅਸਮਾਨਤਾਵਾਂ ਦੇ ਗ੍ਰਾਫਿਕਲ ਹੱਲ ਅਤੇ ਕੰਟਰਾਪੋਜ਼ਿਟਿਵ (contrapositive) ਵਾਲੇ ਸਵਾਲ ਪੱਕਾ ਆਉਂਦੇ ਹਨ। ਇਹ ਸ਼ੁੱਧ ਤਰਕ ਹਨ ਅਤੇ ਬਹੁਤ ਜਲਦੀ ਹੱਲ ਕੀਤੇ ਜਾ ਸਕਦੇ ਹਨ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Solve absolute value (modulus) inequalities.
  • Understand graphical regions represented by linear inequalities.
  • Determine the negation, converse, and contrapositive of logical statements.
  • Identify tautologies and fallacies using truth tables.

Modulus Inequalities and Logic Statements

When solving inequalities, remember the golden rule: multiplying or dividing by a negative number flips the inequality sign. For absolute value (modulus) inequalities, |x| < a translates to -a < x < a. Conversely, |x| > a translates to x < -a OR x > a. In Mathematical Reasoning, a statement 'If p, then q' (p → q) has three important related statements. The Converse is 'If q, then p' (q → p). The Inverse is 'If not p, then not q' (~p → ~q). The Contrapositive is 'If not q, then not p' (~q → ~p). A crucial fact for the exam is that a statement is logically equivalent ONLY to its contrapositive. A Tautology is a statement that is always true, regardless of the truth values of its components.

Graphical solutions for linear inequalities in two variables (ax + by < c) involve drawing the boundary line (ax + by = c) and shading the appropriate half-plane. A solid line is used for ≤ or ≥, while a dashed line is used for strict inequalities (< or >). To determine which side to shade, pick a test point not on the line—the origin (0,0) is usually the easiest choice. Substitute the coordinates into the inequality. If the resulting statement is true, shade the region containing the test point; if false, shade the opposite region. When dealing with systems of inequalities, the solution set is the geometric intersection (the overlapping shaded area) of all individual regions.

Truth tables are the ultimate fallback for any mathematical reasoning question that seems overly convoluted. While drawing a full truth table takes a minute, it guarantees absolute accuracy for finding tautologies (where the final column is all True) or contradictions/fallacies (all False). However, memorizing key logical equivalences can save you this time. For instance, the negation of an 'AND' statement is an 'OR' statement, and vice versa. Negating (p ∧ q) yields (~p ∨ ~q). This is essentially De Morgan's Laws applied to logic. If a question asks for the negation of 'It is raining and I am carrying an umbrella', the correct answer is 'It is not raining OR I am not carrying an umbrella'. Many students incorrectly pick the 'AND' option here.

Common Trap

A common logical trap is assuming that the converse of a true statement is also true. For example, 'If it is a dog, it is an animal' is true, but the converse 'If it is an animal, it is a dog' is false. Only the contrapositive holds the same truth value.

Worked Example

Q: Find the contrapositive of the statement: 'If a number is divisible by 9, then it is divisible by 3.'

Step 1: Identify the hypothesis (p): 'A number is divisible by 9.'

Step 2: Identify the conclusion (q): 'It is divisible by 3.'

Step 3: The format is p → q.

Step 4: The contrapositive format is ~q → ~p.

Step 5: Write ~q: 'A number is NOT divisible by 3.'

Step 6: Write ~p: 'It is NOT divisible by 9.'

Step 7: Combine: 'If a number is NOT divisible by 3, then it is NOT divisible by 9.'

Answer: If a number is not divisible by 3, then it is not divisible by 9.

🧠 How ERB Asks It

They frequently test modulus inequalities with quadratics inside, e.g., |x2 - 4| < 5. You must split this into -5 < x2 - 4 < 5, solve the two parts separately, and find the intersection.

Quick Recap

  • Flip the inequality sign when multiplying/dividing by a negative.
  • |x| < a means -a < x < a.
  • Contrapositive of (p → q) is (~q → ~p).
  • A statement is logically equivalent to its contrapositive.
  • Tautology = Always True; Fallacy = Always False.
