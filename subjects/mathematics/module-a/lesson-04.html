Lesson 04: Determinants & System of Equations

ਡਿਟਰਮੀਨੈਂਟ ਅਤੇ ਸਮੀਕਰਨਾਂ ਦੀ ਪ੍ਰਣਾਲੀ

Why this matters (ERB 2022 Pattern)

Cramer's rule applications or identifying singular matrices (where the determinant is exactly zero) are staple questions in Master Cadre exams. These test your ability to quickly evaluate square matrices.

ਕਰੈਮਰ ਦਾ ਨਿਯਮ ਜਾਂ ਸਿੰਗੂਲਰ ਮੈਟ੍ਰਿਕਸ ਦੀ ਪਛਾਣ ਕਰਨਾ (ਜਿੱਥੇ ਡਿਟਰਮੀਨੈਂਟ ਜ਼ੀਰੋ ਹੁੰਦਾ ਹੈ) ਪ੍ਰੀਖਿਆ ਵਿੱਚ ਆਮ ਸਵਾਲ ਹਨ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Calculate the determinant of 2x2 and 3x3 matrices rapidly.
  • Use fundamental properties of determinants to simplify heavy calculations.
  • Solve systems of linear equations using Cramer's Rule.
  • Understand the relationship between determinants and matrix inverses.
  • Identify singular and non-singular matrices.

Core Concepts and Exam Focus

The determinant is a unique scalar value that can be calculated from any square matrix. It holds several powerful properties: If any two rows or columns are identical or proportional, the determinant |A| is exactly 0. The determinant of a product is the product of determinants: |AB| = |A||B|. A matrix is classified as singular if |A| = 0, which fundamentally means it does not possess an inverse matrix. To find the inverse of a non-singular matrix, we use the formula: A-1 = 1|A| * adj(A). Therefore, if |A| is 0, the fraction is undefined.

Calculating determinants for 3x3 matrices using Sarrus' rule or cofactor expansion is standard, but you should always look to simplify the matrix first using elementary row operations. Adding a multiple of one row to another does NOT change the value of the determinant. This is your most powerful tool. Aim to create a row or column with mostly zeros before expanding.

The Adjoint matrix, adj(A), is the transpose of the cofactor matrix. A critical property linking a matrix, its determinant, and its adjoint is A * adj(A) = |A| * I. Taking the determinant of both sides yields |A| * |adj(A)| = |A|n, which simplifies to the vital formula: |adj(A)| = |A|n-1. Memorize this; it is guaranteed to appear in the Master Cadre exam.

Cramer's Rule uses determinants to solve systems of linear equations. For a system of three variables, x = Dx/D, y = Dy/D, and z = Dz/D, where D is the determinant of the coefficient matrix, and Dx is the determinant formed by replacing the x-column with the constant terms. If D is not 0, the system has a unique solution and is consistent.

However, the analysis of systems where D = 0 is where examiners separate average students from top scorers. If D = 0, and at least one of Dx, Dy, or Dz is non-zero, the system is inconsistent and has no solution. The planes do not intersect at a single point or a common line. If D = 0 AND Dx = Dy = Dz = 0, the system usually has infinitely many solutions (dependent system), though strictly speaking, it requires further checking to ensure it isn't inconsistent in a specific parallel plane scenario.

Understanding the geometric interpretation helps immensely. A 3x3 determinant calculates the volume of the parallelepiped formed by its row vectors. If the determinant is zero, the volume is zero, meaning the three vectors lie in the same flat plane; they are linearly dependent. This spatial intuition provides a safety check for your algebraic calculations.

Worked Example

Q: Find the determinant of a 2x2 matrix A with first row [2, 4] and second row [1, 3].

Step 1: Identify the elements: a=2, b=4, c=1, d=3.

Step 2: Apply the 2x2 determinant formula: (a * d) - (b * c).

Step 3: Calculate the primary diagonal: 2 * 3 = 6.

Step 4: Calculate the secondary diagonal: 4 * 1 = 4.

Step 5: Subtract: 6 - 4 = 2.

Answer: The determinant is 2.

🧠 How ERB Asks It

Watch out for questions asking for the determinant of the adjoint matrix: |adj(A)|. You do not need to calculate the adjoint itself! Simply use the formula: |adj(A)| = |A|n-1, where n is the order of the matrix.

Quick Recap

  • |A| = 0 means the matrix is singular (no inverse).
  • Proportional rows/cols mean |A| = 0.
  • Scaling property: |kA| = kn|A|.
  • Adjoint determinant: |adj(A)| = |A|n-1.
  • Inverse formula: A-1 = (1/|A|) * adj(A).
