Lesson 05: Arithmetic & Geometric Progressions

ਅੰਕਗਣਿਤਕ ਅਤੇ ਰੇਖਾਗਣਿਤਕ ਪ੍ਰਗਤੀ (AP/GP/HP)

Why this matters (ERB 2022 Pattern)

Questions on finding the sum of infinite geometric series and identifying the nth term of a harmonic progression are staple MCQs. Speed here is crucial.

ਅਨੰਤ GP ਦੇ ਜੋੜ ਅਤੇ ਹਾਰਮੋਨਿਕ ਪ੍ਰਗਤੀ (HP) ਦੀ 'n'ਵੀਂ ਟਰਮ ਲੱਭਣ ਵਾਲੇ ਸਵਾਲ ਆਮ ਹਨ। ਇੱਥੇ ਰਫ਼ਤਾਰ ਬਹੁਤ ਜ਼ਰੂਰੀ ਹੈ।

By the end of this lesson, you will:

  • Calculate the nth term and sum of n terms for Arithmetic Progressions (AP).
  • Calculate the nth term and sum of n terms for Geometric Progressions (GP).
  • Evaluate the sum of an infinite Geometric Series.
  • Understand Harmonic Progressions (HP) as the reciprocal of AP.
  • Apply the AM-GM-HM inequality to solve minimum/maximum problems.

Understanding Progressions: AP, GP, and HP

An Arithmetic Progression (AP) is a sequence where the difference between consecutive terms is constant, known as the common difference 'd'. The nth term is given by a + (n-1)d, and the sum of n terms is (n/2)[2a + (n-1)d]. A Geometric Progression (GP) multiplies each term by a common ratio 'r'. Its nth term is a(rn-1), and the sum of n terms is a(1-rn)/(1-r) for r < 1. A critical concept often tested is the infinite geometric series, whose sum converges to a1 - r, provided the absolute value of r is strictly less than 1. A Harmonic Progression (HP) is simply a sequence whose reciprocals form an AP. There is no general formula for the sum of an HP, which is a common trick question.

Delving deeper into series, the method of differences is an incredibly powerful tool for finding the sum of series that are neither strictly AP nor GP. By subtracting the series from a shifted version of itself, you can often reveal a hidden GP that is easy to sum. Furthermore, recognizing standard sums like the sum of the first n natural numbers, squares, and cubes is mandatory. Sigma notation is heavily used in the exam to condense these expressions, so you must be fluent in distributing the sigma operator across algebraic terms. When evaluating complex limits in calculus later, these series summation formulas will reappear, proving their foundational importance.

The AM-GM inequality is a secret weapon for solving optimization problems without resorting to derivatives. If you need to find the minimum value of an expression like x + 1/x for positive x, simply apply AM ≥ GM. The arithmetic mean is (x + 1/x)/2, and the geometric mean is the square root of (x * 1/x), which equals 1. Thus, (x + 1/x)/2 ≥ 1, leading directly to x + 1/x ≥ 2. The minimum value is 2, achieved precisely when the terms are equal (x = 1/x). Mastering this shortcut will save you precious minutes on the exam, bypassing lengthy differentiation processes.

Common Trap

Applying the infinite GP sum formula when |r| ≥ 1. The series diverges, and the formula yields a nonsensical, incorrect result. Always verify the ratio first.

Worked Example

Q: Find the sum of the infinite geometric series: 2, 2/3, 2/9, 2/27, ...

Step 1: Identify the first term 'a': a = 2.

Step 2: Calculate the common ratio 'r' by dividing the second term by the first: (2/3) / 2 = 1/3.

Step 3: Verify that |r| < 1. Since 1/3 < 1, the series converges.

Step 4: Apply the infinite sum formula: S = a / (1 - r).

Step 5: Substitute values: S = 2 / (1 - 1/3) = 2 / (2/3) = 3.

Answer: The sum is 3.

🧠 How ERB Asks It

ERB frequently tests the relationship between the Arithmetic Mean (AM), Geometric Mean (GM), and Harmonic Mean (HM). Memorize the inequality: AM ≥ GM ≥ HM, and the equation GM2 = AM × HM.

Quick Recap

  • AP nth term: a + (n-1)d
  • GP sum (infinite): a / (1-r) for |r| < 1
  • HP is the reciprocal of AP
  • AM ≥ GM ≥ HM
  • GM2 = AM × HM
